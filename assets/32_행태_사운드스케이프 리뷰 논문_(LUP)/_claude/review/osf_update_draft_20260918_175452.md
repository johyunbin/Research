## 0. Justification For Update

This update aligns the registration with how the review was actually conducted, as reported in the manuscript prepared for submission. It records the completed review stage, the software used, how citation tracking and the OpenAlex supplementary search were run, screening and extraction procedures as carried out, the September 2026 retrieval and assessment of full texts that had not been obtained in August, changes to quality appraisal, the four meta-analysed clusters and the analysis rules applied, the sensitivity analyses performed, registered procedures that were not carried out, and the one analysis component added after registration (planning evidence strength). The original registration remains available unchanged, and the same deviations are reported in the manuscript.

## 1. Review Methods › Type of review

Update (September 2026): Four clusters met the rule of at least three comparable effect sizes and were meta-analysed: walking speed, staying (dwell time), social interaction, and sound–behaviour correlations (17 contributing studies; one of them, found in the September 2026 full-text retrieval, was added to the sound–behaviour correlation cluster). The registration anticipated walking speed and dwell time; the social-interaction cluster and the sound–behaviour correlation cluster, which combines correlations across behavioural domains and both directions, were formed under the same rule when effect sizes were computed. Space use/visitation could not be pooled because the studies did not report variance information, and other outcomes did not reach three comparable effect sizes and were synthesised narratively.

## 2. Review Methods › Current review stage

Update (September 2026): Searches, screening, full-text assessment, citation tracking, screening of the OpenAlex supplementary set, data extraction, quality appraisal, synthesis, and meta-analyses have been completed, and the manuscript has been prepared for submission. Full texts not obtained in August 2026 were sought again and assessed in September 2026, and the synthesis and meta-analyses were updated accordingly. The final corpus comprises 113 included studies and 9 sensitivity-only studies.

## 3. Review Methods › Software

Update (September 2026): Rayyan and ASReview were not used; records, screening decisions, and extracted data were managed with Python scripts and CSV files. The meta-analyses were run in Python 3.13 (NumPy 2.2, SciPy 1.17) with a purpose-written implementation of REML estimation and the Hartung–Knapp adjustment; R (metafor) was not used.

## 4. Review Methods › Overlapping authorships

Update (September 2026): The review has a single author. One study by the author (Jo & Jeon, 2020) met the inclusion criteria and was included in the narrative synthesis; it did not contribute to any meta-analysis. Because there was no second reviewer, its screening and extraction decisions were not cross-checked by another person.

## 5. Search Strategy › Other search strategies

Update (September 2026): Citation tracking was run once through OpenAlex, using as seeds the 84 studies that the database searches yielded as included or sensitivity-only in August 2026; studies added later through citation tracking, the OpenAlex search, or the September full-text retrieval were not themselves tracked. It returned 2,073 records not already in the pool (413 backward, 1,660 forward). Because most were off-topic, a title-level automated prefilter that was not specified at registration was applied: a record was kept for manual screening when its title contained terms from at least two of the three registered concept blocks and it was not an animal or bioacoustic study (428 records), and the other 1,645 records were excluded at title level. This prefilter can miss eligible studies whose titles do not mention behaviour. The OpenAlex supplementary set was screened on 6 August 2026, after an internal audit found that it had not yet been screened: after duplicates were removed, 329 records were screened and 15 were sought for full text; 2 were not journal articles, 13 were assessed, 4 were included, and 1 was classified as sensitivity-only. The reference lists of adjacent reviews were not screened.

## 6. Search Strategy › Results of contacting authors

Update (September 2026): No authors were contacted. Studies whose effect-size ingredients could not be recovered from the published report were kept in the narrative synthesis and excluded from the meta-analyses.

## 7. Screening › Screening stages

Update (September 2026): In August 2026, full texts could not be obtained for 89 of the 189 records sought from the database searches, 25 of the 79 sought from citation tracking, and 8 of the 13 journal articles sought from the OpenAlex supplementary search. In September 2026 these records were sought again through open-access copies (OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Europe PMC) and copies obtained by the author, and the 111 that were obtained were assessed with the same criteria and procedure: 16 were included, 5 were classified as sensitivity-only, and 90 were excluded. Eleven records remained unavailable and were not assessed: nine behind paywalls or pay-per-view, one open-access record without a downloadable copy, and one without a DOI that could not be located. Full-text assessment followed the same AI-assisted procedure as title/abstract screening, with every assessment read by the author.

## 8. Screening › Screener instructions

Update (September 2026): Recurring boundary cases at full-text assessment were resolved with rules applied to all such records. Studies relating residential noise exposure to physical activity not tied to a specific public space were excluded under the setting criterion; during the September assessment, one study included in August was found to have this structure and was re-classified as excluded. Studies reporting only behavioural intentions were classified as sensitivity-only, as registered. Reverse-direction studies whose outcomes were the perception or assessment of the soundscape were included, consistent with the registered reverse-direction outcomes. Studies in which the acoustic environment entered only as a control variable were excluded. In the citation-tracking branch, laboratory or VR reproductions of outdoor scenes were also classified as sensitivity-only, whereas in the database branch such studies stayed in the main synthesis; none of the studies classified by this rule contributed to any meta-analysis. Language eligibility was confirmed from the full text rather than from index metadata, because some records indexed as English were published in Chinese, Korean, or Japanese.

## 9. Screening › Screening reliability

Update (September 2026): The author read every AI pre-classification (1,316 title/abstract records and all full-text assessments); no classification was changed. The planned re-screening of a random 10% subsample after an interval was not carried out, so intra-rater consistency was not quantified for the August screening. For the 111 records assessed in September, 50 records (all records first judged included or sensitivity-only, and the excluded records whose judgement was borderline) were assessed a second time in a separate AI-assisted assessment that did not see the first. Agreement on included or sensitivity-only versus excluded was 92% (Cohen's κ = 0.84); because both assessments were AI-assisted, this is not an inter-reviewer reliability estimate. The five records whose three-category judgements differed were resolved against the full text, and the final judgements were read and accepted by the author.

## 10. Extraction › Extraction stages

Update (September 2026): No separate pilot on 10 studies was run. Effect statistics were extracted verbatim together with their location in the source text, and conversion to effect sizes was done only at the synthesis stage.

## 11. Extraction › Extraction reliability

Update (September 2026): The AI-assisted extraction was read and accepted by the author, including the extraction for the studies added in September. The planned value-by-value check against the source PDFs and the 10% re-extraction were not carried out. Errors in effect-size inputs found later during the review of the analysis code were corrected against the source texts (see Synthesis reliability).

## 12. Synthesis And Quality Assessment › Planned data transformations

Update (September 2026): Detailed effect-size rules were fixed in a separate analysis-rules document before effect sizes were computed; two items were later handled differently from those rules, as described below. When a study provided several effects for the same analysis, one was selected by priority (objective measure, primary analysis, full sample), and effects of equal priority were combined within the study assuming a correlation of 0.5. Only one study per shared sample was used. For repeated observations, the number of participants rather than observations was used as the sample size. The rules reserved studies reporting only observation units for sensitivity analyses, but two social-interaction studies that reported only video-excerpt units were entered in the main analysis with those units as the sample size. Odds ratios from 2 × 2 frequencies were converted to d with Chinn's method. The same conversion, not covered by the rules, was applied to one staying study that reported an adjusted odds ratio from logistic regression for a one-unit increase in a continuous natural-sound index. Missing group sizes were assumed equal, missing standard deviations were recovered from test statistics or standard errors, and variances were derived from reported confidence intervals where sample sizes were not reported; a sensitivity analysis excluded the inputs that relied on assumed group sizes or recovered test statistics. Effects from studies added in September whose unit of analysis, contrast, or sound variable matched a cluster definition only in part were not entered in the main analyses and were used only in sensitivity analyses.

## 13. Synthesis And Quality Assessment › Missing data

Update (September 2026): Authors were not contacted (see Results of contacting authors).

## 14. Synthesis And Quality Assessment › Quality assessment

Update (September 2026): RoB 2 was not applied. Full-text reading identified only two randomised experiments, too few for a separate risk-of-bias subset, and their weaknesses are reported through the MMAT criteria for randomised controlled trials. MMAT categories were assigned from the full text rather than from design labels in the extraction sheet. Studies were rated high (4–5 criteria met), moderate (3), or low (0–2). Thirteen studies from citation tracking whose text had been truncated during automated text extraction were re-appraised from the complete full text; five changed rating (four upward, one downward), and the re-appraised ratings are used. The 21 studies added in September (16 included, 5 sensitivity-only) were appraised from their complete full texts in the same way.

## 15. Synthesis And Quality Assessment › Synthesis plan

Update (September 2026): Random-effects models estimated tau-squared by REML and applied the Hartung–Knapp adjustment to the confidence intervals; 95% prediction intervals are also reported. The narrative synthesis is reported as study counts by behavioural domain and sound-source category (evidence map), by direction of the studied relationship, and by behavioural measurement method; a harvest plot and vote counting of effect directions for studies outside the meta-analyses were not produced. The planned moderator and subgroup analyses (setting type, study design, behavioural measurement method, and direction) were not performed because no cluster had more than seven effect sizes. An analysis component not in the registration was added: exposures manipulated or measured in the included studies were grouped into planning levers, each given a planning evidence strength (high, moderate, low, very low) based on the number of contributing studies, their MMAT ratings, whether pooled confidence intervals excluded zero, the diversity of settings, and robustness in sensitivity analyses. This is a pragmatic judgement by the single author, not a GRADE certainty-of-evidence assessment.

## 16. Synthesis And Quality Assessment › Criteria for conclusions / inference criteria

Update (September 2026): Results are reported with effect sizes, 95% confidence and prediction intervals, and two-sided p values, and the manuscript describes pooled estimates whose confidence intervals exclude zero (p < 0.05) as statistically significant.

## 17. Synthesis And Quality Assessment › Synthesis reliability

Update (September 2026): There were no co-authors, so no co-author review of the analysis outputs took place. After the analyses were completed, the analysis code was reviewed for errors with an AI-based code review. The review found four errors: tau-squared estimated by maximum likelihood instead of REML, two effects from one study entered separately in the walking-speed cluster despite the one-effect-per-study rule, two input values in the correlation cluster that did not match the source studies, and a rank-correlation sensitivity analysis that excluded nothing. All were corrected and all meta-analyses were recomputed; the corrections did not change the conclusions.

## 18. Synthesis And Quality Assessment › Publication bias analyses

Update (September 2026): No meta-analysed cluster reached 10 effect sizes (the largest had seven), so funnel plots, Egger's test, and trim-and-fill were not performed, and publication bias was not assessed further.

## 19. Synthesis And Quality Assessment › Sensitivity analyses / robustness checks

Update (September 2026): Performed: (1) excluding MMAT low-quality studies (not possible for walking speed, where all contributing studies were rated low); (2) excluding laboratory or VR reproductions of outdoor scenes (identical to the main analyses, because none contributed to them); (3) including behavioural-intention-only studies (possible for social interaction only, where one sensitivity-only study found in September reported a behavioural-intention outcome); (4) leave-one-out for each cluster. Added: recomputing sample sizes by observation units, excluding rank correlations, excluding reconstructed inputs, an alternative walking-speed definition that adds a plaza background-music study, and excluding studies found by citation tracking. Added after the main analyses: adding a study that reported visit frequency instead of dwell time (staying), adding a study whose observation records were not independent (social interaction), and adding studies found in September whose effects matched a cluster definition only in part (one study comparing quiet-signage conditions for walking speed; one study relating site-average crowd density to respondent-level data and two studies rating sound importance or preference for sound–behaviour correlations). Not performed: (5) the fixed-effect versus random-effects comparison.
