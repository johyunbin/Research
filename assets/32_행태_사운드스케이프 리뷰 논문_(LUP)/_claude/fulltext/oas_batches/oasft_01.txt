Abstract: This study is grounded in Merleau-Ponty's phenomenology of perception and investigates 
how the concepts of the “phenomenological body” and “intentionality of consciousness” intervene in 
the perception and meaning-making processes of urban square spaces. Dongfanghong Square in 
Lanzhou, China, was selected as the empirical case, through which the research systematically explores 
citizens’ sensory experiences, emotional responses, and identity formation in the context of their actual 
spatial use. In-depth interviews were conducted with 20 citizens to collect narratives of their spatial 
experiences. The data were analyzed using NVivo qualitative analysis software through a three-phase 
coding process—open, axial, and selective coding—and thematically examined to derive a dynamic 
structural pathway that progresses from perception to emotion and ultimately to identity. The findings 
reveal a structural tension in the sensory experience of Dongfanghong Square. While the square exhibits 
initial visual attraction, it lacks visual focal points and symbolic depth. In the tactile dimension, hardened 
pavement materials and insufficient microclimatic modulation reduce citizens’ willingness to linger. 
Auditory and olfactory environments are dominated by traffic noise and lack natural scents, thereby 
limiting the space’s capacity to induce emotional comfort. Although the square serves multiple practical 
purposes, it is characterized by a deficiency in interactive engagement. Furthermore, the analysis shows 
that memories, social experiences, and symbolic associations deeply influence citizens’ emotional 
responses and cultural identity related to the square. Expressions of nostalgia for past rituals—such as 
flag-raising ceremonies and feeding pigeons—and criticism of the current absence of cultural 
symbolism underscore the lack of emotional identity and meaning construction within the space. 
Theoretically, this study expands the interpretive potential of phenomenology of perception in the 
context of urban public space research. It empirically validates the sequential chain of bodily perception, 
emotional activation, and symbolic meaning generation. Methodologically, it demonstrates the 
effectiveness of qualitative thematic analysis in capturing complex spatial experiences. Practically, the 
findings suggest the necessity of reconstructing the experiential system of urban squares along three 
dimensions: enhancing multisensory environmental design, fostering behavioral interaction, and

요약: 본 연구는 메를로-퐁티의 지각 현상학을 이론적 기반으로 삼아 ‘현상적 신체’와 ‘의식의

논의를 전개한다. 이를 위해 중국 란주시의 동방홍광장을 실증 사례로 선정하고, 시민들이 
실제 사용 장면에서 경험하는 감각 체험, 정서 반응 및 공간 정체성 형성과정을 체계적으로 
고찰하였다. 연구는 20명의 시민을 대상으로 심층 인터뷰를 실시하여 공간 이용 경험을 
수집하였으며, NVivo 질적 분석 도구를 활용하여 개방 코딩, 축 코딩, 선택 코딩 과정을 거쳐 
주제 분석법으로 ‘지각 - 정서 - 정체성’으로 이어지는 동적 구조 경로를 도출하였다. 연구 
결과에 따르면, 동방홍광장은 감각 체험 차원에서 구조적 긴장을 드러낸다. 시각적으로는 
일정 수준의 초기 흡인력을 지니고 있으나, 시각적 초점과 상징적 층위가 결여되어 있다. 촉각 
차원에서는 지면의 경화와 미기후 조절 요소의 부족으로 인해 시민들의 체류 의지가 
약화되었으며, 청각과 후각 환경에서도 차량 소음이 지배적이고 자연의 냄새가 결핍되어 있어 
정서적 안정을 유도하는 기능이 제한적이었다. 행동 측면에서는 이용 목적이 다양함에도 
불구하고 상호작용성이 부족한 특징을 나타냈다. 나아가 분석을 통해 시민들의 공간 정서 
반응과 문화 정체성은 과거의 기억, 사회적 경험, 상징 체계와의 연결성에 크게 의존하고 
있음이 밝혀졌다. 특히 국기 게양식, 비둘기 먹이주기와 같은 역사적 활동에 대한 향수와 
현재의 문화적 상징 부재에 대한 비판은 광장이 정서적 정체성과 의미 구성 측면에서 결핍이 
존재함을 부각시킨다. 본 연구는 이론적 측면에서 지각 현상학이 도시 공공공간 체험 연구에 
적용될 수 있는 해석의 폭을 확장하였으며, ‘신체적 지각, 정서적 발생, 의미적 생성’이라는 
연속적 사슬의 존재를 실증적으로 입증하였다. 방법론적으로는 복잡한 공간 경험 데이터를 
처리함에 있어 질적 주제 분석법의 유효성을 제시하였고, 실천적 측면에서는 다감각적 환경 
개선, 행동 상호작용 지원, 문화적 이미지 삽입이라는 세 가지 방향에서 도시광장의 체험 
시스템을 재구성할 필요성을 제안하였다. 본 연구의 결과는 향후 도시 공공공간의 인간 중심 
설계 및 문화 갱신을 위한 이론적 참조와 실천적 방향성을 제공할 수 있을 것이다. 
 
핵심어: 지각 현상학, 도시광장, 신체 지각, 다감각적 체험, 정서 반응, 공간 정체성, 문화 기억 
 
1. 서론 
1.1 연구 배경 및 필요성 
도시화가 가속화됨에 따라 공공공간의 질은 도시의 지속가능성을 평가하는 핵심 
요소로 부상하고 있다. 도시광장은 공공공간의 주요 구성 요소로서, 단순한 사교, 문화 
교류, 공공활동의 집합지일 뿐만 아니라 시민들이 만나고, 교류하며, 여가를 즐기고 일상 
속에서 도시를 체험하고 지각(知覺)하는 공간이자, 도시 이미지 형성의 중요한 공간적 
거점으로 기능한다. 더불어 광장은 도시 정체성 형성과 브랜드 가치를 강화하는 데에도 
뚜렷한 기여를 한다는 연구들이 제시되고 있다[1]. 현대 도시광장은 일반적으로 상업활동 
중심 광장, 문화예술 중심 광장, 역사상징적 광장 등 다양한 유형으로 구분된다[2][3].

부각되고 있다. 
 
1.2 선행연구 
현재까지 도시광장에 대한 선행연구는 주로 기능적 평가나 조형적 미학에 집중되어 
있으며[6], 시민의 신체 지각, 정서 반응, 공간 기억과 같은 심층적 체험 메커니즘에 대한 
논의는 상대적으로 부족한 실정이다[7]. 예를 들어, 중국 도시광장의 조형물에 관한 
연구는 장소성 형성에 긍정적 영향을 미친다는 점을 지적하였으나, 이는 표면적인 
상징적 의미에 국한되어 있었다. 또한 역사문화광장에 대한 연구 역시 인지적 경로에 
초점을 맞추고 있을 뿐, 지각(知覺) 체험 메커니즘에 대한 구체적 탐색은 미비하였다[8]. 
국제 연구에서도 Ridings와 Chitrakar는 호주 브리즈번의 도시광장을 분석하여, 인간 
중심의 설계 요소(인간적 스케일, 가장자리 활성화, 시각적 연계성 등)가 사회적 
상호작용을 촉진함을 강조하였다[9]. 
한편, 란주 동방홍 광장에 대한 환경 디자인 연구는 다수 진행되어 왔으나 여전히 몇 
가지 주요한 한계가 존재한다. 중국 내 연구는 주로 광장의 최근 개조 현황, 녹지 설계 
전략, 공간 스케일 디자인, 지역문화 반영 등의 측면에 집중되어 있으며[10][11], 국제 
연구 역시 역사문화 개념, 지하상업공간 설계, 교통 및 공간 분포 등에 국한되어 
있다[12][13]. 종합적으로 볼 때, 시민의 실제 이용 경험과 체감에 기반한 연구가 
부족하며, 사용자 시각에서의 접근 또한 미흡하다. 특히 동방훙 광장의 환경 디자인 
요소와 시민 지각 체험 간의 관계를 분석한 연구는 전무하며, 심층 인터뷰를 통해 
시민의 신체 지각 체험과 공간 설계 간의 상호작용 메커니즘을 규명한 연구도 부재하다.

2. 이론적 배경 
2.1 지각 현상학 이론 
지각 현상학(Phenomenology of Perception)은 프랑스 철학자 모리스 메를로-퐁티(Maurice 
Merleau-Ponty)가 20세기 중반에 제창한 이론으로 세계를 이해하는 데 있어 추상적 
이성보다 신체의 직접적인 경험을 핵심으로 간주한다. 이는 전통 철학과 달리 주체와 
객체의 이분법을 해체하며, 신체를 지각(知覺)의 주체로 보고 세계의 의미 구성과 공간 
인지에 적극적으로 개입하는 존재로 이해한다. 이러한 ‘신체–지각–공간’ 간의 상호작용 
관계는 현대 도시공간 연구에 중요한 이론적 기초를 제공한다[14][25]. 
 
2.1.1 현상적 신체성과 공간 지각의 생성성 
메를로-퐁티는 ‘현상적 신체(le corps phénoménal)’ 개념을 통해, 신체는 단순히 수동적인 
지각 도구가 아니라 의미 생성을 주도하는 능동적 주체임을 주장한다. 공간 경험은 이에 
따라 정적인 인식이 아닌, 개인의 움직임, 지각, 환경이 지속적으로 상호작용하면서 
이루어지는 동적 과정으로 이해된다[14]. Yong은 장애 여성의 보행 경험을 분석하면서 
익숙한 지팡이를 짚고 이동할 때 촉각이 신체에서 지팡이의 끝까지 확장된다고

2.1.2 의식의 지향성과 지각의 간접성 
메를로-퐁티(Merleau-Ponty, 
1945)는 
후설(Edmund 
Husserl)이 
제시한 
‘의식의 
지향성(intentionality of consciousness)’ 개념을 계승·발전시키며, 인간의 의식 활동이 내면의 
사고에 국한되지 않고 항상 외부의 어떤 대상을 향해 나아간다는 점을 강조하였다[28]. 
다시 말해, 우리의 지각은 항상 ‘무언가를 향한’ 구조를 가지며, 이는 곧 의식이 환경 
속에서 능동적으로 작용하는 경향성을 보여줄 뿐 아니라, 개인의 경험, 문화적 기억, 
정서적 기대가 공간에 투사되는 결과이기도 하다. 메를로-퐁티는 더 나아가, 의식은 
단선적으로 대상을 향하는 것이 아니라, 신체와 세계가 상호작용하는 과정에서 행위와 
감각의 피드백을 통해 끊임없이 확장되는 ‘육화된 지향성(embodied intentionality)’을 
지닌다고 보았다. 
Nam은 도시공간에서 개인이 건축물이나 공간 요소를 지각하는 과정은 단순한 시각적 
인식의 결과가 아니라, 지속적인 이동, 접근, 체류 등 신체적 행위를 통해 주관적 경험과 
문화적 
상징을 
대조하는 
가운데 
점진적으로 
의미를 
형성해 
가는

설명한다[24]. 이처럼 공간에 대한 의미 해석과 지각의 구조는 대부분 간접적으로 
형성되며, 이는 곧 공간 지각이 환경과의 상호작용 속에서 점차적으로 ‘해석되는’ 것이지, 
단순히 ‘보이는 대로’ 인식되는 것이 아님을 뜻한다. 
Rahimi와 Asadi는 정서 차원에서 지각의 지향성을 도시공간에 적용하며, 개인이 새로운

형성하도록 유도한다고 설명한다[29]. 이는 결과적으로 개인의 정서적 회복력과 정신 
건강 증진에 긍정적 영향을 줄 수 있으며, 공간의 불확실성과 낯섦이 오히려 지각 
갱신과 의식 확장의 계기가 될 수 있음을 보여준다.

한편, 지각 현상학은 공간을 정적인 용기(container)로 보지 않고, 신체의 이동과 행위 
경로에 따라 끊임없이 변화하는 동적인 체계로 간주한다. Kim와 Min은 공간 경험은 걷기, 
관찰, 정지 등의 연속 행위 속에서 생성되며, 이러한 흐름성과 반복성이 공간의 
‘현실감’(sense of presence)을 구성한다고 지적하였다[23]. 메를로-퐁티(1945) 또한 공간에 
대한 정서적 반응은 고정된 것이 아니라, 구체적 맥락에 따라 지속적으로 재구성된다고 
설명한다[28]. 예컨대, 하나의 개방된 광장이 적절한 동선 유도나 경계 설정이 부재할 
경우 시민에게 불안과 거리감을 유발할 수 있지만, 공간이 잘 조직되어 있을 경우에는 
안락함과 친밀감 같은 긍정적 정서를 환기할 수 있다. 
또한, 지각 현상학은 공간 지각의 맥락 의존성(context-dependency)을 강조한다. 관광 
경험 연구에 따르면, 메를로-퐁티의 신체 주체성 이론을 적용해볼 때, 관광객의 공간 
체험은 단순한 감각 반응을 넘어, 개인의 기억, 문화적 배경, 정서적 몰입 등 심층적 
요소와 복합적으로 작동함을 확인할 수 있었다. 이처럼 지각 과정은 의향성을 내포하며, 
주관적 경험과의 상호작용을 통해 구성된다. 
결국 지각 현상학의 관점에서 ‘의식의 지향성’은 시민이 구체적 공간 속에서 신체적 
지각 활동, 경험 기억, 정서적 투사를 통해 공간을 간접적으로 인식하는 과정을 설명하는 
핵심 개념이다. 이러한 인식은 외부 자극에 대한 즉각적 반응을 넘어서 개인의 문화적 
정체성과 사회적 경험이 깊숙이 내재된 복합적 과정으로, 공공공간의 지각 메커니즘을 
보다 복잡하고 실제적인 방식으로 설명할 수 있게 해준다. 
종합하자면 지각 현상학 이론은 신체 주체성, 의식의 지향성, 동적인 상황 경험을 
중심으로, 도시광장과 같은 공공공간의 설계가 개인의 지각 체험에 어떠한 영향을 
미치는지를 깊이 이해할 수 있도록 이론적 기반을 제공한다. 이 이론은 도시 공공공간 
디자인의 이론적 깊이를 풍부하게 할 뿐 아니라, 실질적인 공간 설계에서 인간 
중심적이고 체험 기반적인 연구 경로와 방법론을 제공한다는 점에서 의의가 크다. 
 
2.2 도시광장 공간 디자인 요소 
미래 예술 융합적 관점에서 바라본 도시광장의 환경 디자인 요소(인공 및 자연 요소)는 
더 이상 단순한 물리적 구조물로 인식되지 않는다. 오히려 그것은 시민의 신체 감각 
체계를 통해 활성화되는 ‘경험의 장(場)’으로 해석된다. 시각, 촉각, 청각, 후각 등 다중 
감각적 지각(知覺)은 개인이 공간을 전체적으로 인식하는 감성적 틀을 형성하며, 이는 
‘현상적 신체’가 도시광장을 즉각적으로 반응하는 방식으로 작동한다. Kim, Gong 와 
Park은 인간의 공간 인식이 신체 지각 기관과 공간 요소 간의 직접적 상호작용에서 
비롯된다고 하였으며, 특히 촉각은 질감과 깊이를 인지하고, 후각은 환경 변화에 대한 
민감한 반응을 통해 신체와 공간의 공생 관계를 더욱 강화한다고 설명한다[22]. 이러한 
관점에서 건축물, 포장재, 식재, 수체 등 공간 구성 요소는 단지 물리적 기반에 그치지 
않고, 정서를 자극하고 행위를 유도하는 매개체가 되며, 이는 곧 메를로-퐁티가 강조한 
‘신체는 곧 존재’라는 지각의 출발점을 구체적으로 반영하는 것이다[30]. 따라서 란주주 
동방홍 광장과 같은 공공공간에서 환경 디자인의 가치는, 시민의 신체가 실제 공간에 
존재하며 감각적으로 상호작용하는 지각 과정에서 재조명되어야 한다. 
동시에, 도시광장의 인공 및 자연 요소는 ‘의식의 지향성’ 측면에서도 깊은 영향을 
미친다. 조명 시설, 안내 시스템, 공공 예술, 생태 식재 등 디자인 구성 요소는 상징적 
의미와 문화적 지향을 내포하고 있으며, 시민으로 하여금 정서와 기억을 구체적인 공간

기여하였다. 참여자 유형에는 일상적 운동 이용자, 자녀 동반자, 일시적 휴식자, 사진 및 
예술 애호가, 광장 활동 참여 시민 등 여러 성격의 이용자들이 포함되었다. 모든 
참여자는 자발적 동의 하에 인터뷰에 응하였으며, 연구 목적, 익명성 원칙, 데이터 활용 
방식 등에 대한 설명을 듣고 사전 동의를 한 후 연구에 참여하였다. 이러한 방식은 
신체적 경험과 공간적 상호작용의 폭넓은 사례를 확보함으로써 연구의 심층성과 
신뢰성을 높이는 데 기여하였다. 
 
3.2 자료수집 및 분석방법 
자료 분석은 Braun과 Clarke의 주제 분석법(Thematic Analysis)을 채택하여 시민들의 지각 
경험 이면에 내재된 구조적 의미를 도출하고자 하였다[35]. 분석 도구로는 NVivo 14 질적 
분석 소프트웨어를 활용하여 의미 노드 구성, 교차코딩, 키워드 동시 출현 탐색 등의 
기능을 기반으로 20명의 인터뷰 텍스트를 체계적으로 코딩하고 주요 주제를 도출하였다.

기여를 한다는 점이 입증된 바 있다[36].

3.2.1 개방 코딩 (Open Coding) 
본 연구에서는 질적 분석 도구인 NVivo 소프트웨어를 활용하여, 각 참여자의 인터뷰 
원자료를 문장 단위로 정밀하게 분석하고 개방 코딩을 실시하였다. 이 단계에서는 
분석자의 선입견을 배제하고, 미리 정의된 범주 없이 열린 태도로 텍스트를 읽어 
내려가며 핵심 개념과 중요한 내용을 식별하는 데 주력하였다. 그 결과, 다수의 자유 
노드(free nodes)가 생성되었으며, 이를 통해 시민들이 공간을 체험하는 과정에서 나타나는 
신체적 지각 접점을 구체적으로 드러낼 수 있었다. 도출된 자유 노드는 시민들이 언급한 
공간의 시각적 인상, 신체와 환경 간의 상호작용 경험, 공간에 대한 정서적 반응(예: 
이완, 즐거움, 불안 등), 디자인 요소에 대한 평가(색채, 재질, 자연 요소 등), 공간 
분위기와 소리 환경에 대한 인상, 환경 냄새에 대한 후각적 체험, 시설에 대한 만족도, 
공간에서의 안전감 및 쾌적성(舒适性)에 대한 판단 등을 포함하였다. 
이러한 지각 내용은 메를로-퐁티가 주장한 ‘현상적 신체’ 개념에 근거하여 해석할 수

3.2.2 축 코딩 (Axial Coding) 
개방 코딩을 통해 도출된 자유 노드들을 바탕으로, 의미적으로 유사하거나 밀접하게 
연관된 개념들을 통합·분류하여 축 코딩을 수행하였다. 이 단계에서는 개별적이고 
단편적으로 존재하던 개념들을 서로 연결하고 조직화하여 보다 구조적이고 통합적인 
분석 틀을 구성하고자 하였다. 특히 노드 간의 인과관계, 계층구조, 연관성을 중심으로 
핵심 범주와 하위 범주를 도출하여 시민의 공간 지각 경험을 다층적으로 이해할 수 있는 
틀을 마련하였다.

후각 등 다양한 감각 채널을 통해 구성되었다. 시각적 매력은 색채 조합과 디자인

3.2.3 선택 코딩 (Selective Coding) 
개방 코딩과 축 코딩을 바탕으로, 본 연구는 메를로-퐁티의 지각 현상학 이론에서 핵심 
개념으로 제시된 ‘현상적 신체’, ‘의식의 지향성’, ‘동적 상황 경험’에 주목하여 선택적 
코딩을 수행하였다. 이를 통해 시민의 신체 지각과 공간 상호작용 간의 심층적 관계를 
구체화하고, 본 연구의 이론적 해석 틀을 구성하였다. 
먼저 ‘현상적 신체’의 차원에서 선택적 코딩은 시민이 공간 내에서 신체를 통해 
직접적으로 수행하는 지각 경험의 구조에 초점을 맞추었다. 시민은 시각, 촉각, 청각, 
후각 등 다양한 감각 채널을 통해 광장 환경과의 최초 접점을 형성한다. 시각적으로는 
상징적 건축물이나 공간의 개방감을 주로 인식하고, 촉각적으로는 지면의 재질과 온도 
변화에 대한 민감한 반응을 보였다. 청각적으로는 환경음과 소음의 구분, 후각적으로는 
자연의 향기나 불쾌한 냄새에 따른 정서적 반응이 나타났다. 이러한 감각 자극은 공간 
지각의 기초적 층위를 구성하며, 이후 시민은 걷기, 정지, 활동 참여, 자녀 돌봄 등 
다양한 신체 행위를 통해 공간과의 동적 상호작용을 점차 확장해 나갔다. 이 과정을 
통해 시민은 단순한 수용자가 아니라, 몸의 움직임과 지각적 작용을 통해 스스로 공간의 
의미를 능동적으로 구축하는 주체로 기능한다는 점이 드러났다[30]. 
한편 ‘의식의 지향성’ 차원에서는 시민이 광장을 사용하는 과정에서 경험하는 문화적 
기억, 정서적 체험, 사회적 의미 생성 과정에 초점을 맞추었다. 인터뷰에서 드러난 
‘즐거움’, ‘이완’, ‘안전감’과 같은 심리 상태나 과거 기억에 기반한 역사적 동일시, 문화적 
소속감, 공동체 교류에 대한 인식은 의식이 어떻게 공간의 상징성과 정서적 층위에 
지향성을 부여하고 있는지를 보여준다[31]. 이는 곧 개인이 과거의 경험과 기억을 현재의 
지각 체험에 통합함으로써 공간에 대한 인식과 정체성을 재구성하는 과정을 반영하며, 
메를로-퐁티가 강조한 바와 같이 ‘현상적 신체’가 지각 행위를 통해 공간 의미를 
생성하는 구체적 사례로 해석될 수 있다. 
이러한 분석을 통해 도시광장은 신체의 개입과 정서의 의향이 복합적으로 교차하는 
‘사회적 장(場)’으로서 끊임없이 구성되고 있음을 확인할 수 있다. 시민의 지각 행위는 
물리적 자극을 넘어서 정서적, 상징적 차원의 의미 생성으로 확장되며, 도시 공간은 이 
과정 속에서 개인적·집단적 정체성과 기억을 수용하는 살아 있는 장소로 기능하게 된다. 
다음은 지각 현상학 이론에 기초한 선택 코딩 결과를 요약한 표이다[표 2]. 
따라서 지각 현상학의 이론적 틀 아래, 개방 코딩에서 축 코딩, 선택 코딩에 
이르기까지의 과정을 통해 시민이 도시광장에서 신체적 지각을 출발점으로 하여 행동 
참여와 경험 투사를 거쳐 궁극적으로 공간 인식과 정서적 지향을 생성해 나가는 통합적 
경로가 단계적으로 그려졌다. 이에 따라 제4장에서는 이러한 코딩 구조를 바탕으로 
인터뷰 자료에 대한 구체적인 분석을 전개하고자 한다.

4.1 현상적 신체와 다감각 경험의 공간 반응 메커니즘 
메를로-퐁티의 지각 현상학 이론에 따르면, ‘현상적 신체’는 단순히 공간을 수동적으로 
지각하는 존재가 아니라, 환경과의 지속적인 상호작용 속에서 공간의 의미를 능동적으로 
생성해내는 지각 주체이다[28][14]. 본 연구는 선택 코딩 결과를 바탕으로, ‘감각 경험과 
신체 상호작용을 통해 정서적 반응에 이르고, 궁극적으로 공간 의미를 투사하는’ 하나의 
통합된 공간 지각 경로를 도출하였다. 이에 따라 본 절에서는 시각, 촉각, 청각, 후각 등 
다양한 감각 체계를 중심으로, 시민들이 ‘현상적 신체’를 통해 동방홍 광장 환경과 
상호작용하면서 경험한 구체적인 체험 및 공간 의미 생성 메커니즘을 분석한다. 
 
4.1.1 시각적 지각과 장소 인식의 형성 
시각은 시민들이 광장과 최초로 접촉하는 주요 감각 통로이다. 인터뷰 결과에 따르면, 
다수의 시민들은 광장의 시각적 인상에 대해 가장 먼저 반응을 보였으며, 이는 단순한 
물리적 공간 인식을 넘어 ‘공간 분위기’에 대한 정서적 반응으로 이어졌다. 시민 G는 
“가장 인상 깊은 건 연단(主席台) 구역이다. 첫눈에 딱 봤을 때 굉장히 웅장했고 시선을 
확 끌었다”고 말했으며, 시민 I는 “체육관이 굉장히 디자인적으로 독특하다. 건물이 살짝 
비스듬히 보이는데 시각적으로 꽤 강한 충격을 주고 인상이 깊다”고 언급했다. 이처럼 
‘첫눈에 느껴지는 충격’은 단순한 시각 정보 제공을 넘어서, 공간 의미 형성의 출발점이 
됨을 보여준다.

지각 경험이 ‘공허하다’는 인상을 받았다고 응답하였다. 시민 E는 “광장 바닥이 전부

4.1.2 촉각적 체험과 신체적 쾌적성의 피드백 
촉각은 가장 직접적인 신체 반응 경로로서, 시민들의 공간 체험에서 중요한 역할을 
수행한다. 인터뷰에 참여한 시민들 다수는 동방홍 광장의 지면 재질과 미기후(微氣候) 
조절 측면에서 명확한 불편함을 호소하였다. 시민 E는 “광장 바닥이 전부 딱딱한 회색 
벽돌로 되어 있어서, 오래 걸으면 발이 피곤하고 불편하다”고 말했고, 시민 K는 “비 오는 
날에는 바닥이 고르지 않고 물이 고이기도 해서 미끄럽다. 편하게 걸을 수 없어 늘 
긴장된다”고 응답하였다. 시민 O는 “겨울에는 바람이 너무 세게 불어서 피할 곳이 없다. 
광장 전체가 바람막이가 없어서 걷기 힘들다”고 지적하였다. 이처럼 광장은 재질 선택, 
기후 대응, 스케일 조정 등의 측면에서 사용자 중심적 설계가 결여되어 있으며, 이는 
시민들의 체류 의사와 행동 자연성에 부정적인 영향을 미치고 있었다. 
촉각은 단지 물리적 반응에 국한되지 않고, 공간과의 정서적 관계 형성에도 깊이 
관여한다[22]. 광장의 촉각 환경이 유발하는 불편함은 시민들에게 정서적 거리감과 공간 
소외감을 불러일으켰다. 시민 H는 “광장이 너무 딱딱해서 친근한 느낌이 없고, 따뜻한 
감각이 부족하다”고 말했으며, 시민 G는 “손으로 만졌을 때도 차갑고, 별로 접촉하고 
싶다는 생각이 들지 않는다”고 응답했다. 시민 A는 “지금 광장에선 돌과 시멘트가 
대부분이고, 주변 상업 시설도 많아서 전체적으로 별로 안 편안하다. 체험이 별로 좋지 
않다”고 단언하였다. 이러한 서술은 광장의 환경이 ‘차가운 재질감’과 ‘생경한 지각’으로 
인해 신체적 수준에서 시민들과의 정서적 연결을 형성하지 못하고 있음을 시사한다. 
촉각 체험의 구조적 결핍은 시민의 공간 소속감을 약화시키는 동시에, 광장이 도시 
공공공간으로서 수행해야 할 사회적 기능 역시 제한하고 있는 것으로 분석된다. 
 
4.1.3 청각 환경과 공간의 안전감 
도시광장의 소리 환경은 시민의 정서 조절과 공간 신뢰감 형성에 중요한 역할을 한다. 
본 연구에서 다수의 시민은 동방홍 광장 주변의 교통 소음이 심각하여, 공간 내에서 
이완감을 느끼기 어렵다고 응답하였다. 시민 C는 “광장 주변에 차가 너무 많고, 차량 
소음이 심하다. 광장에서 대화하려 해도 너무 시끄럽다”고 말했으며, 시민 F는 “귀 
옆에서 들리는 건 전부 차 소리다. 특히 사거리 근처에선 안전하다는 느낌도 없고, 전혀 
긴장이 풀리지 않는다”고 언급하였다. 시민 M은 “광장 안에 들리는 소리는 거의 없고, 
그냥 차 소리나 사람 소리뿐이라 단조롭다”고 지적하였다. 이러한 반응은 동방홍 광장이 
자연적이고 부드러운 청각 요소가 결여되어 있어, 시민에게 조용하고 친근한 휴식 
분위기를 제공하지 못하고 있음을 보여준다. 
Serino 외는 신체와 소리 환경의 다중 경로 상호작용이 ‘신체 경계감’을 형성하며, 이는 
개인의 정서 판단과 행동 방식에 직결된다고 설명한다[19]. 동방홍 광장의 경우, 소리의 
유도 기능과 공간 기능 분할에 따른 청각적 단서가 부족하여, 청각 경험이 지속적으로 
압박감과 소음에 지배되는 상태에 머물고 있었다. 이는 공간의 정서적 조절 기능을 
약화시키고 시민의 공간 소속감 형성을 저해하는 요인으로 작용하였다. 이러한 점은 
시민 B의 진술에서도 확인된다. 그는 “물소리나 배경 음악 같은 게 있으면 훨씬 덜 
시끄러웠을 텐데, 지금처럼 차 소리만 계속 들리면 안에서 굉장히 답답하다”고 하였다. 
이는 동방홍 광장이 소리 환경을 통해 지각 유도나 정서적 위안을 제공하지 못하고 
있으며, 청각적 공간 설계의 구조적 결핍이 존재함을 의미한다.

4.1.4 후각 지각과 정서 환기 메커니즘 
후각은 도시 공간 설계에서 종종 간과되지만, 무의식적인 정서 자극과 기억 활성화 
측면에서 대체 불가능한 기능을 지닌다[29]. 동방홍 광장은 후각 차원에서 뚜렷한 결핍을 
보이며, 시민에게 정서적 환기를 일으킬 수 있는 공간적 향기 인상을 제공하지 못하고 
있었다. 시민 D는 “광장에서는 식물 향 같은 건 거의 안 느껴지고, 오히려 일부 구역에선 
약간 기름 냄새가 난다”고 하였으며, 시민 K는 “다른 도시광장에는 분수가 있어서 
가까이 가면 수증기 냄새도 나는데, 여긴 너무 건조해서 그런 섬세한 감각은 거의 
없다”고 언급했다. 시민 E는 “예전엔 잔디가 많아서 전체적으로 초록빛이 강했는데, 
지금은 줄어서 그런 냄새도 더 이상 없다”고 회고하였다. 이러한 진술은 광장 내 자연 
향기 부족으로 인해 발생하는 ‘지각 단절’ 현상과 함께, 기억 체계와의 정서적 공감 
메커니즘이 약화되고 있음을 보여준다. 
지각 현상학은 공간의 의미가 단순한 물리적 구조에 의해 결정되는 것이 아니라, 신체 
경험과 문화 기억의 상호작용 속에서 형성된다고 본다[28]. 특히 후각은 가장 연상력이 
뛰어난 감각 중 하나로, 그 결핍은 감각 인상의 깊이를 약화시킬 뿐 아니라, 공간과 
개인의 정서 기억 사이를 연결하는 다리 역할을 수행하지 못하게 만든다[19]. 동방홍 
광장은 식물 향기, 물기, 흙 냄새 등 상징적인 자연 냄새 요소의 부재로 인해, 시민이 
공간과 ‘지각 공감의 장’을 형성하기 어렵게 되었으며, 결과적으로 기억을 환기하거나 
정서를 이끌어낼 수 있는 능력을 상실하고 있었다. 이는 향후 도시 공간 설계에서 
후각을 구체적인 ‘육화된 지각 시스템’의 일부로 인식하고, 향기를 통한 정체성 인식 및 
문화적 귀속감 형성 기능을 적극적으로 활용해야 함을 시사한다. 
요컨대, 시민 인터뷰를 바탕으로 한 현상적 신체 감각 경험과 광장 공간에 대한 지각 
메커니즘의 구체적인 내용은 [표 3]에 제시되어 있다.

4.2 의식 지향성과 공간 정체성 형성 경로 
메를로-퐁티의 지각 현상학 체계에서 '의식의 지향성'은 지각 행위가 고립적으로 
존재하는 것이 아니라 언제나 ‘무엇인가를 향하고 있음’을 강조한다. 인간은 외부 자극을 
단순히 수용하는 존재가 아니라, 신체적 경험을 바탕으로 정서 상태, 생활 경험, 문화 
기억, 사회적 맥락을 통합하여 공간에 의미를 부여한다[24]. 본 연구의 심층 인터뷰 
결과와 선택적 코딩을 기반으로, 본 절에서는 정서 반응, 기억 소환, 문화 정체성 세 
가지 층위에서 시민들이 도시광장 공간에서 의식의 지향성을 통해 어떻게 주관적 의미와 
귀속 경험을 구축하는지를 구체적으로 분석한다. 
 
4.2.1 정서 경험과 공간 관계의 감정화 형성 
동방홍 광장의 개방감과 일부 공간 요소는 시민의 정서적 공명을 유도하며, 이는 
공간과 개인 간의 심리적 연결성을 강화하는 역할을 한다. 시민 T는 “특히 마음에 드는 
건 넓은 공간이에요. 한 바퀴 도는 것만으로도 기분이 상쾌해지고, 사람들 구경하고 
산책하는 게 편안하다”고 진술하였다. 시민 D 또한 “사람이 많고 음악 공연 같은 게 
있을 때 분위기가 좋고, 마음이 편안해진다”고 말하였다. 이러한 긍정적 피드백은 공간이 
단순한 행동의 배경이 아니라 정서 해방과 심리 조절의 매개로 작용함을 보여준다.

4.2.2 기억 활성화와 경험 흐름의 상징적 메커니즘 
의식 지향성의 핵심은 기억이 '정황 생성의 매개체'로 작용하여 공간의 의미를 
심화시키는 데 있다[29]. 다수의 시민은 광장을 설명하면서 과거 활동에 대한 기억과 
감정적 회상을 자연스럽게 드러냈다. 시민 D는 “어릴 때 비둘기에게 먹이를 주던 기억이 
가장 그립다. 그땐 정말 활기찼는데, 지금은 너무 썰렁하다”고 말했고, 시민 Q는 “예전엔 
국기 게양식이나 마라톤 행사가 자주 열렸는데, 요즘은 그런 행사가 줄어서 기억도 점점 
옅어지는 것 같다”고 언급했다. 이들의 발언에서 드러나는 '그리움', '이전과의 단절' 등의 
정서는 공간 기억의 단절이 정체성 형성에 부정적인 영향을 미친다는 사실을 보여준다. 
Seamon은 '장소 애착'이란 반복적 경험 흔적과 문화적 사건 기억 위에서 형성된다고 
지적한 바 있다[18]. 도시광장이 그 역사적 기능과 상징적 활동을 지속하지 못할 경우, 
공공적 정체성 담지체로서의 역할이 약화될 수 있다. 동방홍 광장은 과거에는 집단 
기억의 중심이었으나, 점차 감각적 경험이 빈약한 단순 통과 공간으로 변화하고 있으며, 
이는 공간의 ‘의미 상실’이라는 구조적 문제를 반영한다. 
 
4.2.3 문화 정체성의 결핍과 인지 투사의 단절 
의식의 지향성은 개인의 기억을 넘어서, 문화 구조를 통해 상징적으로 수용되고 
활성화되어야 한다. 그러나 동방홍 광장을 묘사한 많은 시민은 문화적 상징성 부족에 
대한 당혹감을 표출하였다. 시민 A는 “이름은 동방홍 광장이지만, 특별한 문화 분위기는 
느껴지지 않는다. 그냥 광장일 뿐이다”고 말했으며, 시민 E는 “기억에 남는 지점이 별로 
없다. 그냥 지나가는 곳일 뿐, 정서적으로 남는 게 없다”고 지적하였다. 
Kim와 Min은 공간의 문화 정체성이 자연발생적이기보다는 ‘상징물과 문화 매개체’를 
통해 개인의 가치 공감을 유도해야 한다고 본다[23]. 조형물, 기념비, 문화 안내판 등이 
결여된 동방홍 광장은 시민들의 의식적 투사 대상이 부재한 상태이며, 이는 공간의 
사회적 상징성과 문화 정체성의 이중 결핍으로 이어지고 있다. 
일부 시민은 기능의 동질화가 문화적 기억 형성의 다양성을 억제한다고 지적하였다. 
시민 D는 “여긴 특별한 역사적 흔적이 별로 없어서 외지인이 와도 특별한 볼거리가 
없다”고 말했다. 이는 Nam 이 언급한 바와 같이, 공간에 대한 인지는 신체, 기억, 문화가 
맥락 속에서 복합적으로 생성되는 것이며, 문화적 이미지와 정체성 지시가 결여된 
공간은 사회적 기억의 지속성과 공동체 정체성 유지에 실패한다는 점을 다시금 
보여준다[24].

정서 환기를 위한 공간 분위기, 지속적인 문화 이벤트, 상징물의 배치 등이 부족하여 
시민의 공간 정체성 형성이 어려운 상태에 놓여 있다. 본 분석은 지각 현상학이 제시한 
'신체–정서–기억–문화'의 도시 공간 인지 생성 경로를 보완하며, 향후 도시광장 설계에 
있어 경험 기반의 명확한 설계 근거와 이론적 방향을 제시한다. 
 
4.3 지각 구조에서 공간 의미의 구성 메커니즘 
앞선 두 절의 분석을 통해 확인할 수 있듯이, 시민의 동방홍 광장 공간 경험은 단순한 
감각 반응이나 행동적 상호작용에 국한되지 않으며, 보다 심층적인 의식 투사와 문화적 
정체성의 형성 메커니즘까지 포괄한다. 지각 현상학은 공간에 대한 인지와 의미 구성이 
추상적 사고에 의해 이루어지는 것이 아니라, 구체적인 신체 경험, 감각 지각(知覺),

그리고 상황적 연결성 속에서 지속적으로 전개된다고 강조한다[28]. 이에 따라 동방홍 
광장은 도시 중심의 공공공간으로서, 시민들에게 지각되고, 이해되며, 인정되는 전 
과정이 ‘현상적 신체’로부터 출발하여, 다감각적 경험과 사회적 행동을 통해 촉발되고, 
다시 의식의 지향성에 의해 공간 의미로 확장되는 절차를 따른다. 
구체적으로, 본 연구의 심층 인터뷰 자료를 바탕으로 지각 구조와 공간 의미 간의 
생성 경로를 다음과 같이 통합 정리할 수 있다. 
첫째, 시각·촉각·청각·후각 등 다감각 경로는 시민이 광장에 진입할 때 경험하게 되는 
초기 접점이자 감각적 문턱으로 작용하며, 이는 즉각적인 정서 반응에 직접 영향을 
미친다. 예를 들어, 시민 g는 “처음 보는 순간 연단이 굉장히 인상 깊었다”고 표현하였고, 
시민 e와 f는 각각 “바닥이 딱딱하다”, “차 소리가 불안하다”, “식물 향이 느껴지지 
않는다”고 응답하였다. 이러한 감각 기반의 부정적 지각 경험은 정서 반응을 제약하며, 
이는 Seamon이 말한 '지각적 내장성' 개념과 부합한다[18]. 즉, 공간에 대한 정서 반응은 
감각 환경의 조화성과 신체의 현존감에 의존한다는 것이다. 
둘째, 광장의 문화 기능이 상징물이나 인식 가능한 이미지로 구체화되지 못할 경우, 
정서 투사와 문화적 정체성의 형성 또한 약화된다. 예컨대, 시민 d는 “어릴 때 
비둘기에게 먹이를 주던 기억이 있다”고 말하며 개인적 기억의 장소화를 표현한 반면, 
시민 a와 e는 “문화적인 분위기가 없다”, “기억에 남을 만한 지점이 없다”고 지적하였다. 
이는 Rahimi 와 Asadi가 말한 ‘기억 활성화 메커니즘’이 공간 내 문화적 표현 요소와 
밀접하게 연결되어 있음을 보여준다[29]. 상징적 문화 매개체가 부재한 경우, 시민의 의식 
지향성은 투사 대상을 상실하게 되며, 공간 의미의 지속적 구축이 어려워진다. 
이상의 분석을 바탕으로, 본 연구는 동방홍 광장의 공간 인지 구조를 다음과 같은 
경로 모델로 요약할 수 있다. 시민은 우선 감각 자극을 통해 광장 경험을 시작하고, 이후 
공간 내에서의 행동 참여를 통해 정서 반응을 유도하며, 그 결과 의식의 지향성이 
발동되어 궁극적으로 공간 정체성을 형성하게 된다. 이 경로 모델은 지각 현상학이 
주장한 ‘신체–의식–세계’의 상호작용 메커니즘을 충실히 반영하며[28], 또한 Nam이 
제시한 “건축적 의미는 주체와 객체 간 상호작용 속에서 형성된다”는 관점과도 
일치한다[24]. 
요컨대, 동방홍 광장의 공간 의미는 미리 규정된 것이 아니라, 시민이 신체 지각, 행동 
참여, 정서 체험, 문화 투사의 전 과정을 통해 점진적으로 구성해 나가는 것이다. 이 
과정은 지각 현상학의 핵심 명제인 ‘공간은 육화된 경험에 의해 사회적으로 구성된다’는 
이론을 실증적으로 검증하며[14], 향후 도시광장 환경 설계에 있어 다음과 같은 함의를 
제공한다. 시민의 ‘현상적 신체’ 차원에서의 지각 통합성, ‘의식 지향성’ 차원에서의 투사 
가능성, 그리고 공간 구성 요소가 지닌 ‘의미 형성 사슬’ 내 연결 기능을 종합적으로 
고려할 필요가 있다. 이로써 비로소 ‘지각 가능하고, 참여할 수 있으며, 정체성을 형성할 
수 있는’ 공공공간 경험 경로가 실현될 수 있을 것이다.

ABSTRACT 
The purpose of this research is to investigate the complex dynamics of noise pollution 
at the University of Adrar-Algeria, with a particular emphasis on the impact that the 
dry desert climate has on the acoustic environment of the campus. With the goal of 
cultivating an environment that is characterised by tranquilly, comfort, and optimal 
conditions for attention, learning, and practical engagement, the research attempts to 
achieve the goal of enhancing the existing discourse on noise management inside 
academic institutions. The inquiry makes use of a methodical approach in order to 
investigate the many climatological characteristics that are unique to the desert 
climate and that lead to increased noise levels within the university grounds. In 
addition, the research investigates the ways in which noise emissions from various 
sources alter inside the confines of Adrar University, taking into account the ways in 
which the harsh desert climate influences the parameters that control these 
emissions. The investigation of human behaviour patterns is relevant to this 
investigation. In particular, the investigation will focus on instances of protracted 
assemblage in air-conditioned places, which correlates with increased noise levels. In 
light of the fact that noise control is an essential component of the academic 
environment, the research being conducted at the University of Adrar includes the 
collection of questionnaires, the administration of on-site sound measurements, and 
the creation of an all-encompassing noise map. The findings shed light on the climatic 
factors that are primarily responsible for the excessive amount of noise that exceeds 
the standards that are suggested by the World Health Organisation (WHO). 
 
Keywords: noise, university, desert climate, Adrar, noise mapping.

1 INTRODUCTION 
Deserts, constituting 40% of the world's continents and accommodating 
20% of the global population, have garnered heightened attention due to their vast 
energy potential (solar, wind) and rich mineral and groundwater resources. Despite 
their apparent inhospitality, deserts, exemplified by the Sahara, play a pivotal role 
in the strategic establishment of infrastructure, including universities, in North 
Africa. The arid desert climate, characterized by deficient and erratic rainfall, 
elevated temperatures, and persistent winds, poses unique challenges for 
sustaining life in such environments. Notably, the proliferation of oil sites in North 
African deserts, coupled with historical, cultural, scientific, and ethnic dimensions, 
has spurred the growth of universities in these regions. However, this development 
has introduced a contemporary concern noise pollution. The escalating global 
apprehension regarding noise pollution, recognized as a hazardous form of 
environmental pollution, has prompted regulatory interventions by authoritative 
bodies such as the World Health Organization (WHO). The WHO has set 
standards and limits for noise levels on university campuses to foster serene 
learning environments (Berglund et al., 1995). Noise pollution within university 
premises is a multifaceted challenge, with varied sources contributing to its 
manifestation. These sources encompass ambient noise from activities, human 
voices, electronic devices, and construction activities. The impact of noise on the 
effectiveness of learning, teaching activities, and overall student performance is 
well documented (Bronzaft, 1981; Cohen et al., 1973; Connolly et al., 2015; 
Thompson et al., 2022). As a microcosm of diverse activities, the university 
campus exhibits numerous factors influencing noise generation and propagation. 
These include climatic conditions and the number of students, both of which play 
pivotal roles in noise pollution dynamics. In desert-region universities, the 
prevalent use of air conditioning, coupled with extreme temperatures, dictates 
student movements and affects the utilization of communal spaces, contributing to 
the overall acoustic landscape (Tadjeddine, 2023; Bendjillali, 2023). Amidst the 
imperative of cultivating a calm and conducive learning environment, noise 
management within universities becomes paramount. This involves a spectrum of 
measures, encompassing sound insulation, spatial planning, organizational 
strategies, and educational initiatives to maintain acceptable noise levels.

Studies in Engineering and Exact Sciences, Curitiba, v.5, n.1, p.2333-2365, 2024 
The situation at Adrar University in southwest Algeria reflects an alarming 
rise in noise pollution concerns, prompting an investigative approach. This study 
undertakes a series of noise level measurements within Adrar University, 
scrutinizing existing noise sources and particularly monitoring key desert climate 
parameters responsible for heightened noise levels within the university. New 
developments in research methods, like using simulation software to map noise 
and look into different ways to reduce noise in buildings and cities, show that 
proactive noise control strategies are becoming more important (Tadjeddine, 2023; 
Aletta et al., 2015; Baclet et al., 2022; Matteis et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2022; Xu 
and Liu, 2022). This research contributes to the broader objective of evaluating the 
impact of desert climates on noise pollution on university campuses. Focusing on 
the case of Adrar University, situated in a desert environment, this study addresses 
a notable gap in the existing literature by juxtaposing noise levels in tranquil desert 
settings with those on the bustling university campus. Additionally, it seeks to 
identify and analyse factors contributing to heightened noise levels within the 
university context, thereby enhancing our understanding of noise pollution 
dynamics in desert-based educational institutions. 
The main objective of this work is to investigate the dynamics of noise 
pollution within the University of Adrar in Algeria, with a specific focus on 
understanding the influence of the arid desert climate on the campus acoustic 
environment. The research aims to enhance the existing discourse on noise 
management within academic institutions, with the goal of cultivating an 
atmosphere characterized by serenity, comfort, and optimal conditions for 
concentration, learning, and practical engagement. The study examines the 
various climatological parameters inherent to the desert climate that contribute to 
increased noise levels, as well as the impact of human behavior patterns on noise 
emissions within the university premises. The findings from this research are 
intended to inform the development of proactive noise control strategies for desert-
based educational institutions.

The Algerian regulations indicate that a decision should be made to 
characterise the local noise. Still, they do not stipulate a minimum time to collect

equipment (one-minute measurements were taken at random intervals of three, 
fifteen, and sixty minutes). This test determined the minimum amount of time 
required for accurate measurements. In those situations, the pressure sound 
recording was made as soon as the obtrusive sound. 
 
2.4 NOISE MAPPING AND METHODOLOGY ADOPTED 
In accordance with recent guidelines from the European Environment 
Agency (EEA) emphasizing the management of areas with favorable 
environmental noise quality, the study incorporated noise mapping as a pivotal 
analytical approach. The methodology employed for noise mapping involved the 
utilization of ArcGIS 10.8 software, coupled with the Inverse Distance Weighted 
(IDW) interpolation technique within the Spatial Analyst Module. This robust 
 
 
 
 
2346 
Studies in Engineering and Exact Sciences, Curitiba, v.5, n.1, p.2333-2365, 2024 
approach facilitated the analysis of data and the subsequent generation of noise 
level maps across diverse measurement points within the study area. 
 
2.4.1 Methodological steps 
1. Guidelines from European Environment Agency (EEA): 
• the study adhered to the recommendations outlined by the EEA for 
managing areas with suitable environmental noise quality. 
2. Utilization of ArcGIS 10.8 Software: 
• ArcGIS 10.8, a widely recognized Geographic Information System (GIS) 
software, was employed for its versatile spatial analysis capabilities. 
3. Inverse Distance Weighted (IDW) Interpolation Technique: 
• the IDW interpolation method was chosen for its effectiveness in estimating 
values at unsampled locations based on the proximity and weightage of 
neighboring data points; 
• this technique is acknowledged in scientific research for its robustness in 
spatially interpolating data, particularly in scenarios involving noise level 
assessments. 
4. Spatial Analyst Module Integration: 
• the IDW interpolation technique was integrated within the Spatial Analyst 
Module of ArcGIS 10.8, enhancing the software's capability to perform 
spatial analyses and generate noise level maps. 
5. Assessment and Visualization of Noise Levels: 
• the IDW interpolation facilitated the accurate assessment and visualization 
of noise levels across the entire study area; 
• the spatial distribution of noise pollution was effectively captured, providing 
valuable insights into the variability and concentration of noise levels. 
By adopting this comprehensive methodology, the study achieved a 
nuanced understanding of the spatial distribution of noise pollution within the 
university environment. The integration of noise mapping, ArcGIS 10.8 software, 
and the IDW interpolation technique served as a robust framework for spatial 
analysis, enabling informed decision-making and mitigation strategies related to 
noise pollution.

3.6 NOISE MAPPING OF THE UNIVERSITY OF ADRAR 
The noise mapping of the University of Adrar employed a simulation 
methodology integrating real-time noise data and an advanced multidimensional 
model of the university campus. The noise data utilized in the simulation were

derived from outdoor measurements conducted across various classes and districts, 
providing a comprehensive representation of the ambient acoustic environment. 
These data points were meticulously integrated into Geographic Information System 
(GIS) software, specifically leveraging the capabilities of ArcGIS version 10.8. The 
variables extracted from the measurements were employed to construct a prediction 
surface utilizing the Kriging interpolation method. 
The discerned elevated noise levels were notably attributed to the 
operational activities of the air conditioning systems and ongoing maintenance 
endeavors throughout the academic season. Notably, these activities, crucial for 
maintaining optimal conditions amid high temperatures and sandy winds, are 
constrained from execution during vacation periods. 
Additionally, our observations revealed that a significant portion of inter-
building transportation within the university occurs via automobiles, emerging as a 
predominant source of noise pollution this source as known to be the most 
important source of noise in urban settings. On-site data collection encompassed 
floor areas and building facades, and a systematic distribution of one hundred point 
receptors at a consistent height of 1.6 meters was executed within the study area. 
Figure 22 visually encapsulates the noise map portraying the existing acoustic 
conditions before any intended intervention. The depiction showcases a prevalent 
exposure of the majority of the study area to sound levels surpassing 65 dB(A), 
with specific locations falling within the ranges of 51 to 69 dB and 70 to 85 dB (A). 
The outcomes derived from the noise mapping substantiate the imperative 
for planned interventions aimed at mitigating the impact of noise pollution sources 
and instituting solutions to cultivate a more conducive sonic environment for the 
university campus. Prospective interventions include meticulous landscaping and 
campus planning to curtail noise propagation, alongside the integration of 
soundproofing materials in the construction of buildings, classrooms, and 
dormitories. The strategic plantation of trees, hedges, and bushes is advocated to 
serve as natural noise-reducing barriers.

4 CONCLUSION 
In this study, the analysis of the different climatological parameters 
(temperature, humidity, precipitation, wind speed) gives a precise idea of the 
desert climate, which is characterised by a high annual temperature and by an 
alternation of sandy winds of varying speed depending on the time of Year, with 
low or even non-existent humidity in certain areas. These parameters have a 
significant effect on pollution sound inside the campus of the university; we have 
concluded that: 
• the overall noise level inside the rooms and building at Adrar University is 
unacceptable; 
• the most crucial noise sources inside the university are the movement of 
vehicles, student activities, and construction activities; 
• the temperature and certain climatic factors that characterise the desert 
region, such as the winds, cause students to extend their time inside the 
 
 
 
 
2361 
Studies in Engineering and Exact Sciences, Curitiba, v.5, n.1, p.2333-2365, 2024 
rooms due to the availability of air conditioners and cooling systems. These 
cause high background noise inside the classrooms; 
• the increased noise levels have been considered a contributing factor to 
university students' decreased academic performance. Therefore, it is 
imperative to prioritise establishing a favourable acoustic environment 
within university buildings and their environs to reduce noise levels; 
• the noise measurement results in the urban desert environment of Adrar, 
Tamantit and Timimoune show that the desert environment is calmer, 
favourable for living well, resting and having a better quality of life; 
• exposure to loud noise was 10 a.m. to 12 a.m., coinciding with the best 
climate parameters; 
• the hot desert climate does not encourage the authorities to schedule work 
outside teaching time for practical and health reasons of the workers. 
The social survey results show that more than 83% of respondents think 
that the noise recorded inside Adrar University negatively affects and impacts 
students' academic performance. The Desert environment's calm and tranquillity 
made people sensitive to low noise levels. For this reason, 51% of respondents 
were sensitive to the lowest noise level. 55% of the subjects answered that there 
is no acoustic comfort inside the University of Adrar. However, 45% of the 
respondents were annoyed during the practical sessions, 30% during the courses, 
and 25% at rest places. 
On the other hand, regarding the impact of noise sources on individuals, the 
responses are 28% for voice and communication, 26% for construction and 
renovation work, and 22 %for noise generated with air conditioning systems. As a 
result, we concluded that there was a significant correlation between meteorological 
parameters and the time when people were more exposed to noise. The 
meteorological parameters play a paramount role; whenever the weather is pleasant, 
the movement of students and university workers peaks, which leads to an increase 
in the noise level inside the university. Finally, we have done noise mapping of the 
University of Adrar to determine the noisiest areas that are less noisy and for the 
planning of the campus university to reduce the spread of noise. Testing architectural 
and urban noise reduction alternatives and promoting adequate acoustic conditions 
depend on joint action between campus planning and room design.

The findings of this research hold significance for both society and 
academia: 
• provide a nuanced understanding of the distinct challenges posed by desert 
climates in exacerbating noise pollution on university campuses; 
• can inform the development of targeted noise mitigation strategies for 
universities in arid regions, enabling more serene and conducive learning 
spaces; 
• results generated can extend benefits to surrounding communities, 
informing noise management approaches in desert-based institutions and 
municipalities; 
• highlights the importance of considering unique climatic and behavioural 
factors when addressing noise dynamics in desert-based academic settings; 
• empower universities, policymakers and urban planners to devise more 
effective noise mitigation strategies, fostering enriching educational 
experiences and enhancing quality of life. 
However, the study is limited in geographic scope, focusing solely on the 
University of Adrar. Further research is needed to explore noise pollution patterns 
in other desert-based universities to validate generalizability. 
Future work should integrate advanced noise modelling, incorporate 
longitudinal data, investigate secondary health/well-being effects, and explore 
economic/environmental impacts of mitigation strategies. Addressing these 
limitations can strengthen the evidence base and provide more robust 
recommendations for enhancing acoustic environments in arid academic settings.

Abstract
This study examines how music and spirituality shape everyday wellbeing among
urban informal workers in Ibadan, Nigeria. In a context where market traders
and motor park drivers navigate long and unpredictable workdays, auditory
practices play a crucial role in regulating mood, fostering resilience, and facilitating
social interaction. Despite the centrality of music and spiritual engagement in
daily life, little is known about how these practices are structured and experienced
across time, creating a gap in scholarship on the intersection between musicology
and African urban soundscapes. Using qualitative methods, including in-depth
interviews with 20 participants and non-participant observation in selected markets
and motor parks, the study traces a temporal shift in sonic environments. On one
hand, mornings are dominated by gospel songs, communal prayers, and
exhortations with repetitive melodic and rhythmic structures that provide moral
grounding, optimism, and group cohesion. On the other hand, afternoons shift to
secular genres such as fuji, highlife, and afrobeats, whose polyrhythms, call-
and-response motifs, and cyclical grooves sustain energy, encourage social
interaction, and support coping with the demands of urban labour. Findings reveal
that workers deliberately curate sacred and secular sounds to navigate both
economic and social pressures, promoting personal and collective wellbeing. By
situating music at the intersection of spirituality, work, and resilience, the study
highlights how auditory practices function as both social and affective resources.
This research contributes to African urban ethnomusicology by demonstrating
the structural and emotional dimensions of music in everyday life, and its quotidian
role in shaping individual and communal resilience.
Keywords: Urban soundscapes, music and wellbeing, sacred and secular, Yoruba
spirituality, Informal economy

Introduction
Before the first customer arrives and before money begins to change hands, many urban
workers begin the day by sounding their faith. In markets, motor parks, and roadside shops,
prayer and music are woven into the opening moments of labour, marking the transition from
rest to work and framing the day’s uncertainties within spiritual expectation. Far from being
eccentric, these practices reflect a widely held belief that work is sustained not only by physical
effort but by inner balance and spiritual grounding. Spirituality is thus understood as a
complementary resource that supports workers’ capacity to pursue and maintain organisational
and personal goals, grounded in holistic wellbeing that integrates the physical, mental, and
spiritual (Khanifar et al., 2010). Scholars have shown that religiosity can shape job performance,
often suggesting that spiritually inclined workers are more committed, resilient, and effective
in their duties (Allameh et al., 2011; Othman & Harriri, 2012; Omolawal & Akinwumi, 2020).
Yet, while these discussions have largely focused on formal workplaces, far less attention has
been paid to the informal urban economy, where music and spirituality are embedded in
everyday strategies of survival, sociability, and wellbeing.
This study focuses on Ibadan, one of Nigeria’s oldest and most vibrant urban centres,
where music and spirituality play indispensable roles in structuring daily life and wellbeing. For
market traders and motor park drivers, mornings are typically marked by communal prayers,
gospel songs, and exhortations that establish an atmosphere of spiritual orientation before the
day’s economic transactions unfold. As the hours advance, however, the soundscape gradually
shifts: gospel tunes and sacred melodies give way to secular music, including highlife, fuji, and
afrobeats, reflecting the dynamism of urban experience. This interplay between sacred and
secular not only illustrates the fluidity of religiosity and spirituality but also reveals how sound
is mobilised as a resource for resilience, optimism, and social cohesion.
In African societies, music and spirituality are closely intertwined, shaping how individuals
and communities experience life’s rhythms and challenges. Ayorinde and Ajose (2022) argue
that gospel music in Nigeria functions not merely as religious expression but also as a means
of everyday meaning-making. In this sense, music is both transcendental and practical: it
invokes divine presence while also setting the emotional tone for labour and social interaction.
Similarly, Ajose (2024) highlights how Yoruba Pentecostals engage in creative music-making
on prayer mountains, blending artistry with spirituality to negotiate personal and communal
wellbeing. These studies affirm that music and spirituality are not limited to formal religious
institutions but extend into quotidian spaces such as markets, parks, and households.
Spirituality itself has been theorised in multiple ways. Psaila (2019) suggests that the concept
stems from the recognition that human beings are essentially spiritual, and that spiritual wellbeing
is an integral dimension of holistic health. In Nigerian urban contexts, however, spirituality is
often inseparable from religiosity. Although there are distinctions in scholarly discourse, the
two terms are used interchangeably in this study, recognising their overlapping influences on
behavioural attitudes, work ethics, and interpersonal relationships. This fusion is evident in

https://doi.org/10.53982/agidigbo.2025.1302.15-j Samuel Adejube
Ibadan’s markets and motor parks, where sacred and secular domains constantly interact
(Oyelade, Jegede, Akintunde, & Omobowale, 2021).
The concept of the soundscape is useful for understanding how urban dwellers structure
their day through sonic practices. From the invocation of sacred sounds at dawn to the pulsating
rhythms of fuji at noon, Ibadan’s soundscape reflects the dynamic shifts between spirituality
and secular labour. Ajayi (2024) underscores that fuji music, in particular, is deeply embedded
in the rhythms of everyday Yoruba urban life, blurring boundaries between entertainment,
identity, and livelihood. Likewise, Adejube and Ajala (2024) illustrate how music in Islamic
worship contexts can be ambivalent, simultaneously sacred and contested, revealing broader
tensions in how sonic practices are embedded in social and spiritual life.
In Ibadan’s marketplaces and motor parks, this sacred-secular continuum is not only
audible but performative. Workers consciously structure their days around music and prayer,
using sound both to prepare spiritually and to sustain energy in the face of economic pressures.
This aligns with Igboin’s (2024) observation of prayer cities in Nigeria, where constant prayer
and sonic invigoration contribute to urban resilience and vitality. Such practices suggest that
spirituality and wellbeing are not confined to personal devotion but are negotiated communally
through sound. The concept of wellbeing in Nigerian urban studies has been explored from
multiple perspectives. Rigon (2018) analyses wellbeing in urban Nigeria as multidimensional,
encompassing material, relational, and subjective dimensions. Adegunloye and Afowowe
(2025) further argue that environmental and behavioural conditions in the core areas of Ibadan
significantly shape urban wellbeing, particularly in relation to health, social interaction, and
economic activity. For informal workers such as traders and drivers, wellbeing is precarious,
subject to fluctuating income, stressful labour, and unstable urban infrastructures. Yet through
spiritual practices and music, these workers cultivate resilience, framing wellbeing not simply
as absence of illness but as a state of balance, hope, and social cohesion.
The intersection of wellbeing and spirituality has also been recognised in studies of religious
practices across Nigeria. Omolawal and Akinwumi (2020) contend that religiosity strongly
influences work ethics and performance, while Oyelade et al., (2021) demonstrate how the
sacred and profane coexist in urban marketplaces, shaping interpersonal relations and economic
exchanges. By situating these insights within Ibadan’s sonic environment, this study contributes
to a more nuanced understanding of how wellbeing is enacted in everyday life.
Research Problem and Rationale
Despite the growing literature on music, spirituality, and wellbeing in Nigeria, there remains a
gap in examining how these dimensions intersect in the informal urban economy, particularly
within the soundscapes of markets and motor parks. Previous studies have emphasised either
the role of spirituality in formal workplaces (Allameh et al., 2011; Othman & Harriri, 2012;
Khanifar et al., 2014), or the broader religious and cultural significance of music (Ayorinde &
Ajose, 2022; Ajayi, 2024). Few have explicitly analysed how sacred and secular sonic practices

are sequentially woven into daily routines, and how this structuring of sound contributes to
wellbeing in the midst of economic precariousness.
This study therefore seeks to address three core questions:
1. How do market traders and motor park drivers in Ibadan incorporate spiritual music
into their morning routines, and what meanings do they ascribe to these practices?
2. How does the transition from sacred to secular music across the day reflect the dynamics
of urban life, work, and social interaction?
3. In what ways do these sonic practices contribute to everyday wellbeing, resilience,
and social cohesion among informal workers?
Significance of the Study
By examining these questions, the study contributes to scholarship in three main ways. First,
it expands understandings of urban soundscapes in Africa, highlighting how music functions as
a resource for navigating the rhythms of survival. Second, it underscores the fluidity of spirituality
and religiosity in everyday life, demonstrating how sacred and secular are not dichotomous
but mutually constitutive. Finally, it illuminates the lived strategies of informal workers in Ibadan,
offering insights into how wellbeing is actively negotiated through sonic practices amidst
economic and social challenges.
In doing so, this research builds on and extends earlier works such as those of Oyelade et
al. (2021) on the sacred-profane intersection in urban markets, Ajayi (2024) on fuji and
everyday Yoruba life, and Ayorinde and Ajose (2022) on gospel music as everyday meaning-
making. It also resonates with broader debates in African musicology and urban studies on
the interrelation of sound, spirituality, and survival (Adejube & Ajala, 2024; Ajose, 2024).
Ultimately, this study situates Ibadan’s traders and drivers not only as economic actors but as
cultural agents who “save the day” by mobilising prayer, music, and sonic creativity to foster
optimism, cohesion, and resilience in the city’s vibrant urban life.
Literature Review
Music, Spirituality, and Everyday Wellbeing
African music scholarship consistently demonstrates that sound is central to how everyday
life, belief, and social relations are organised. Spirituality is often framed as a dimension of
wellbeing that extends beyond formal doctrine to include practices through which individuals
cultivate meaning, moral orientation, and emotional balance (Psaila, 2019). In Nigeria, spirituality
and religiosity are closely intertwined, shaping attitudes to work, endurance, and social
responsibility (Omolawal & Akinwumi, 2020). Music operates within this intersection, translating
belief into everyday practice and making spirituality audible within ordinary spaces.
Studies of Yoruba urban culture show that music is inseparable from ethical and social
negotiation. Popular musical forms often articulate tensions around morality, survival, pleasure,

https://doi.org/10.53982/agidigbo.2025.1302.15-j Samuel Adejube
and aspiration rather than offering simple entertainment (Samuel & Adejube, 2019). Adelakun’s
(2024) analysis of prayers and African popular pleasures further challenges rigid sacred secular
boundaries, arguing that religious performance is animated by affect, embodiment, and
enjoyment. Ajose (2025) similarly reflects on Nigerian gospel music discourse, showing how
sacred commitments are continually shaped through engagement with mundane realities.
Together, these studies foreground musical fluidity and situational meaning, offering a useful
lens for understanding soundscapes where prayer, labour, and leisure coexist.
Beyond questions of belief, research highlights the role of spirituality and music in shaping
work practices and resilience. Organisational studies suggest that spiritual orientation enhances
discipline, motivation, and emotional regulation in workplace contexts (Allameh et al., 2011;
Khanifar et al., 2014). While focused largely on formal employment, these insights resonate
with informal urban economies where workers rely on cultural and spiritual resources to cope
with uncertainty. Adejube (2025) situates music within Nigeria’s creative economy, emphasising
its role in sustaining livelihoods, fostering adaptability, and reinforcing cultural continuity. This
perspective underscores how everyday musical practices among traders and drivers are linked
to both economic survival and social cohesion.
Soundscape scholarship provides further insight into these dynamics by treating sound as
socially produced rather than incidental. Adejube, Samuel, and Adeleke (2024) show that
entertainment modes in Nigerian settlements function as tools for social organisation and survival
rather than leisure alone. In urban markets and motor parks, sound structures time, regulates
emotion, and reinforces collective presence. This understanding aligns with studies of religious
and commercial spaces in Ibadan, where prayer, trade, and music intersect seamlessly (Oyelade
et al., 2021). Adejube and Ajala (2024) similarly demonstrate how Islamic worship practices
within NASFAT involve musical negotiation and ambivalence, reflecting broader tensions
between devotion and urban modernity. These works point to sound as a medium through
which urban subjects navigate competing moral and economic demands.
Empirical research on Nigerian music further illustrates how different genres respond to
specific temporal and emotional needs. Gospel music has been shown to provide spiritual
reassurance while supporting everyday meaning making in contexts of economic pressure
(Ayorinde & Ajose, 2022). Fuji music, by contrast, has been situated as a central genre in
Yoruba urban life, mediating humour, endurance, and social interaction (Ajayi, 2024). These
genres are not oppositional but complementary, operating within the same social spaces and
responding to different moments within the workday. Wellbeing in this context is relational
and culturally embedded, shaped by shared practices rather than individual psychology alone
(Rigon, 2018; Adegunloye & Afowowe, 2025). Igboin’s (2024) work on prayer cities further
reinforces the role of sound in generating collective energy and urban vitality.

Gaps and Contribution
Despite this growing body of scholarship, limited attention has been paid to how informal
workers actively organise sound across the workday. Existing studies often examine gospel
or popular music in isolation, focus on formal religious or organisational settings, or overlook
the temporal sequencing of sacred and secular sound. There remains little empirical work on
how traders and motor park drivers in Ibadan deliberately structure their sonic environments
to navigate labour, spirituality, and wellbeing. By examining the movement from prayers at
dawn to secular music later in the day, and by combining ethnographic and musicological
analysis, this study contributes to debates on African urban soundscapes, sacred secular
entanglements, and the role of music in sustaining everyday resilience.
Methodology
Research Design
This study adopts a qualitative research design to examine how music and spirituality shape
everyday wellbeing among urban informal workers in Ibadan metropolis. A qualitative approach
allows for close engagement with lived experience and routine practices, making it possible to
understand how sound is actively used to manage labour, emotion, and social interaction.
Rather than treating music as background noise, the study approaches sound as a meaningful
social practice embedded in everyday work life.
Study Areas
Fieldwork was conducted across three major commercial centres in Ibadan: Bodija Market,
Dugbe Market, and Gbagi Market, alongside adjoining motor parks within these areas. These
locations were selected because they represent different economic zones and social rhythms
within the city. Bodija Market operates as a major food distribution hub with intense early
morning activity, Dugbe Market functions as a central commercial district with dense trading
and transport activity, while Gbagi Market is known for textiles and artisanal goods. Together,
these sites offered rich and contrasting sonic environments in which music, prayer, and labour
intersect daily.
Participants and Sampling
A total of 20 participants were purposively selected for the study. The sample comprised
market traders and motor park drivers who were actively engaged in daily informal economic
activity. Participants included a mix of Yoruba women and men, alongside Igbo male traders
who play a visible role in Ibadan’s commercial life. They were involved in diverse occupations
such as food trading, textile sales, spare parts trading, transport services, and loading work.
This mix allowed the study to capture variations in gendered experience, occupational rhythm,
and ethnic positioning, while also highlighting shared strategies of coping within informal urban
labour.

https://doi.org/10.53982/agidigbo.2025.1302.15-j Samuel Adejube
Data Collection
Data were generated through in depth interviews and non-participant observation. Interviews
were semi structured and conducted in English, Yoruba, and Nigerian Pidgin, depending on
participants’ preference. Most interviews took place within workspaces during less busy
periods of the day. Conversations focused on daily routines, musical choices, spiritual practices,
perceptions of wellbeing, and the emotional and social effects of music across the workday.
Participants were encouraged to reflect on specific songs, genres, and moments when music
felt particularly necessary or meaningful.
Non participant observation complemented interview data and allowed sustained attention
to everyday sound practices. The researcher observed morning prayer sessions, background
music during trading hours, radio use, spontaneous singing, and collective listening practices.
Particular attention was paid to how sound shifted across the day, with mornings often marked
by gospel music and prayer, and later hours dominated by fuji, highlife, and afrobeats.
Observations were recorded as detailed field notes documenting both sonic activity and
participants’ bodily and social responses.
A notable observation occurred in Dugbe Market, where members of the Igbo trading
community organised collective praise and prayer sessions on Thursday mornings. These
gatherings brought together Igbo traders and neighbouring sellers before full trading activities
began. Gospel songs, clapping, and short exhortations created a shared atmosphere of spiritual
preparation and social bonding, highlighting how religious sound also functions as a form of
community organisation within competitive trading spaces.
Data Analysis
The data collected were subjected to content analysis. Also, a musicological analysis of selected
songs frequently played or referenced by participants. The analysis focused on lyrical themes,
rhythmic repetition, call and response patterns, tempo, and performance context. Rather than
formal notation, attention was given to how musical structure shaped affect, participation, and
collective energy. Ethical considerations included informed consent, anonymity, and sensitivity
to participants’ work routines. Care was taken to ensure that research engagement did not
disrupt livelihoods or impose additional burden.
Findings and Discussion
Morning Soundscapes: Prayers, Gospel, and Collective Positivity
In the markets and motor parks of Ibadan, mornings are rarely quiet. From about 6:00 a.m.,
the urban soundscape comes alive with layered activity: traders arranging wares, buses revving
engines, voices exchanging greetings, and, most prominently, prayers and gospel music that
frame the start of the day. For many traders and drivers, work does not begin immediately
with transactions but with a deliberate sonic orientation toward the sacred. Prayers, Yoruba
gospel choruses, and brief exhortations fill the air, functioning as ritual preparation and moments
 662 Àgídìgbo: ABUAD Journal of the Humanities
of social bonding. These practices are structured rather than incidental and reflect an
understanding of wellbeing as a balance of spiritual, mental, and physical readiness, aligning
with Khanifar et al.’s (2014) argument on the complementarity of spirituality and work
performance. Such scenes were observed in Dugbe, where Igbo traders from different shops
gathered every Thursday morning to sing praise songs and engage in churchlike activities
before turning to the day’s business, a practice that coincided with the government designated
environmental sanitation period, which lasted until about 10 a.m.
Certain songs recur across these spaces and have become part of a shared morning
repertoire. Among Igbo traders, songs such as “Good morning Jesus, good morning Lord”
are commonly sung, while Yoruba traders sing or play Yoruba gospel songs on their audio
devices as a way of beginning the day. Sola Allyson’s ‘íjì mo jí lónìí’ (As I woke up this
morning) is frequently played on small loudspeakers or hummed quietly by traders while
arranging their goods. The song’s emphasis on gratitude for life and anticipation of divine
favour resonates with a widespread theology of thanksgiving and hope. Similarly, Beautiful
Nubia’s ‘ÒwúrÍÌ ló jÍì ¹ni máa rír¹ì’ (Morning is the day of one’s fortune) circulates as both
recorded music and quoted lyric, expressing collective optimism that each new day carries
the possibility of success. As Ayorinde and Ajose (2022) note, such gospel and inspirational
songs function as tools for everyday meaning making, orienting labour toward divine favour
while fostering calm, optimism, and social cohesion within shared workspaces.

Shifting Sonic Practices: From Sacred to Secular
As the Ibadan morning matures into late morning and early afternoon, the spiritual tones that
characterise dawn gradually give way to secular rhythms. By around 10 a.m. in markets such
as Dugbe, Oje, and Bodija, gospel music recedes into the background, replaced by the
pulsating beats of fuji, highlife, and increasingly, Afrobeats. This transition does not signal an
abandonment of spirituality but rather reflects a pragmatic recalibration of the city’s sonic
order to match the intensifying demands of urban labour and economic survival. The soundscape
becomes a tool through which productivity, resilience, and sociability are maintained, as secular
music resonates with workers’ lived experiences (Adejube, 2025).
Fuji dominates the mid-morning and afternoon hours, with the booming voices of Pasuma
and Saheed Osupa filling the air from CD sellers’ shops, small kiosks, and bus drivers’ sound
systems. Their music, driven by dynamic percussion and morally loaded lyrics, serves both as
entertainment and social commentary, reflecting resilience, wit, and the improvisatory spirit of
Yoruba urban life. Ajayi (2024) notes that fuji operates as a sonic diary of the Yoruba city
experience, where money, rivalry, morality, and survival are central themes. In the markets,
traders laugh, debate, or sing along to Osupa’s lyrical jabs, while drivers quicken their tempo
in response to the rhythm. Fuji sustains bodily energy and mirrors the competitive and

navigating between sacred and secular soundscapes to remain spiritually anchored yet physically
and mentally mobilised for the day’s labour.

Music, Spirituality, and Everyday Wellbeing
The alternation between sacred and secular soundscapes in Ibadan’s markets and motor
parks illustrates how workers cultivate subjective wellbeing through everyday musical practices.
Drawing on Diener’s (1984) tripartite model of wellbeing—life satisfaction, positive affect,
and low negative affect—this study shows how spiritual and secular repertoires function as
affective resources shaping both inner life and social relations. Morning sacred songs were
strongly linked to life satisfaction, understood as participants’ evaluative judgment about the
quality and direction of their lives. These songs provided a narrative of purpose, affirming that
divine guidance and blessings accompany daily labour. In this way, sacred music contributes
to enduring meaning-making, a core component of life satisfaction (Lawes, 2019; Zoughbi,
2022; Ajose, 2025).
Positive affect, including joy, vitality, and enthusiasm, was more frequently associated with
midday and afternoon secular soundscapes. Traders and drivers identified fuji tracks by Pasuma
and Saheed Osupa as energising, creating lively atmospheres that sustained physical labour
and encouraged social interaction. Similarly, Afrobeats hits from artists such as Asake, Davido,
Wizkid, and Burna Boy animated the environment, making work more pleasurable and
facilitating sociability. Both sacred and secular repertoires also mitigated negative affect,
particularly stress and frustration arising from economic pressures, traffic, or customer disputes.

while fuji and Afrobeats later in the day restored collective cheer and vitality.
In Ibadan, the urban soundscape is a deliberate and dynamic resource for constructing
subjective wellbeing. Music functions as a tool for sustaining optimism, reinforcing social
bonds, and regulating emotional states, illustrating how spirituality and popular culture intersect
to shape everyday life in the informal economy (Lawes, 2019; Zoughbi, 2022). This layered
approach to sound demonstrates that musical practices are integral not only to ritual and
entertainment but also to the psychosocial resilience of urban workers.

Conclusion
In conclusion, the interplay of sacred and secular music in Ibadan’s markets and motor parks
demonstrates that sound is not merely an aesthetic or background phenomenon but a central
resource for everyday wellbeing. Gospel songs, fuji, and Afrobeats each perform distinct yet
complementary functions: they orient workers morally and spiritually, sustain energy and
sociability, and link local struggles to broader cosmopolitan imaginaries. Through Yoruba
proverbs and tonal language, music extends beyond artistic expression to become a medium
for communicating hope, fostering solidarity, and regulating emotional and social life. These
layered soundscapes reveal that urban informal workers deliberately structure their auditory
environments to navigate the challenges of economic precarity, social interaction, and personal
aspiration. The central argument of this study is affirmed: in Ibadan, music at the intersection
of spirituality and labour is a vital, lived technology of resilience, enabling informal workers to
cultivate wellbeing, sustain optimism, and negotiate the complexities of everyday urban life.

ABSTRACT 
The urban space is modulated in its spatial delimitation by qualitative and 
quantitative criteria of the built environment and the various atmospheres in which 
it is situated. This scope and extent, in turn, give rise to a delimitation and a notion 
of environmental marking boundaries, which encompasses the full diversity of 
social practices and behaviors that are more or less profoundly modulated by the 
perceptual aspects of the environment. This is the notion of public space in its 
environmental dimensions. Urban noise can thus be a marking element, a rupture, 
a link, or an articulation between spaces that are morphologically or visually 
incompatible. We can even speak of sonic delimitation of urban space beyond its 
morphological limits, and this delimitation effectively connects the social practice

a fountain as a noise source in the center of a public square clearly supports this 
marking of space and introduces the concept of acoustic space as opposed to 
morphological space, which must now inevitably be reconciled in order to better 
design or manage urban entities. Noise helps to highlight the spatial dimension of 
public space. It even qualifies the space as closed or open and reinforces the visual 
perception of the place. The elements that compose the atmosphere or

resources for improving the orchestration of the world soundscape” (SCHAFER, 
1977). However, over the numerous studies in the field of urban acoustic 
environments, it has become evident that the human dimension is of great 
importance in understanding the issue of urban noise. Sound is one of the five 
major ways that we use to communicate with our surroundings (TAYLOR, 2000). 
Thus, a key distinction between the field of sound environment and 
traditional urban noise studies lies in the central role of human perception (BROWN 
et al., 2011). Social science methods, such as surveys and interviews, complement 
physical sound measurements to capture the subjective perception of sounds 
(PÉREZ-MARTÍNEZ et al., 2018). These approaches increase awareness of

subjective perspectives (PORTEOUS et al., 1985). 
In the other hand, a public space is a space often situated within an urban 
fabric. Whether this space is tangible and physically materialized, or abstract and 
defined by perceptual experience and shaped by socio-cultural relations, it remains 
a whole that is difficult to decompose. Its physical and morphological boundaries 
overlap with its perceptual scope, with social, cultural, and symbolic practices, all 
of which combine to give it shape and existence. Even visual information of the 
location affects noise evaluation (VIOLLON, 2003). It was concluded that the 
influence of visual and verbal information on the auditory evaluation of 
environmental sounds is considerable (ABE et al., 2006). The perceived quality of 
a sound environment is strongly influenced by individual subjective factors, such 
as personal preferences, past experiences, and social and cultural contexts. 
Often, there is a tendency to define a public space by its configuration, 
accessibility, or spatial and morphological components, thus limiting it to a purely 
visual representation. However, it is essential to also conceive of public space as 
an urban entity defined by lived experience and the social activities and practices 
that occur within it. 
We also observe the phenomenon of sonic marking of space, which is very 
present in the daily experience of public spaces. Generally, noise stems from 
human activities, particularly urbanization and the expansion of transportation and 
industry (SINGH, 2004). Various activities and events in urban or public spaces 
give these spaces a specific sound identity. The tram on a boulevard, the fountain

in a square... are very common in the reality of this type of public space in our 
cities. Just like the visual, socio-cultural, or other aspects, sound or noise in public 
space strongly marks its marking boundaries and spatiality by creating a sound 
environment characteristic of the dimensions and shape of that space. It is now 
imperative to adopt a multi-criteria approach to urban phenomena in its complexity 
and diversity. 
To evaluate urban soundscape quality, various methods focus on the 
subjective responses of park users. A study was conducted for a comprehensive 
assessment of soundscapes in four typical urban open spaces in Sheffield, UK, 
using semantic differential analysis (KANG et al., 2010). This technique was 
designed to analyze the emotional connotations of words and has since been 
adapted for a wide range of concepts (OSGOOD et al., 1957). 
In this methodological context, it is often accepted that social interactions, 
which either reinforce or weaken its spatial and morphological or urbanistic 
aspects, take precedence in shaping the real form of public space. A study was 
conducted within a broader context specifically focusing on sound environments in 
urban areas. It was through this study that phenomena of sonic marking and sonic 
delimitation of urban spaces were highlighted. It was also noted that a concept of 
delimitation was established through the social practice and experience of certain 
public spaces under study. 
This paper aims to present certain aspects of that study. A multi-criteria 
approach was used with the objective of better understanding this notion of sonic 
delimitation of urban spaces. The presence of a prominent sound source in most 
of the studied sites provided highly favorable conditions for demonstrating such 
concepts. This paper will also present key case studies on sonic delimitation of 
space through acoustic and perceptual phenomena in urban settings. The study 
focused on urban sites in the cities of Grenoble (France), Setif, and Constantine 
(Algeria).

1.2 RESEARCH CONTEXT 
 
1.2.1 Sound component in urban space 
 
In our daily lives, noise is often regarded as a mere auditory phenomenon. 
However, it is not just the back-and-forth of sound between hearing and the 
environment; it represents the link that creates a relationship between listening and 
the environment. This relationship, which often arises through various acoustic and 
perceptual phenomena, modulates and conditions, particularly through listening 
experiences, our habitual interaction with the environment. As a powerful carrier of 
information, sound either reinforces connections or emphasizes breaks. It is an 
accurate indicator of a specific spatial delimitation and a form of marking 
boundaries in the social and daily use of space. 
It is quite interesting to understand how noise, therefore, conditions the 
spatiality of public places and defines perceptual sub-spaces, which in turn shape 
the lived experience of the area. Through this phenomenon of sonic delimitation, 
urban noise significantly influences interpersonal relationships or those between 
individuals and their surroundings. It is a means by which the spatial, temporal, 
and socio-cultural aspects of a place interact, often clearly defined by the sonic 
environment. 
 
1.2.2 The fountain in public spaces 
 
A portion of this work focused on studying the phenomenon of sonic marking 
in urban spaces. The concept of spatial delimitation through noise appeared more 
evident during the study of a sound source that stands out in the urban 
soundscape. 
The fountain represents the most pronounced and elaborate form of this 
presence. It is also noteworthy how the fountain adds flexibility and articulation to 
a public square in terms of urban morphology, physical and functional organization, 
and especially in shaping the atmospheres within the city. 
Another aspect of water and fountains in public squares, which seems most 
relevant to this study, is their sound. It is one of the key qualities of a fountain that 
 
 
 
 
7 
Studies in Engineering and Exact Sciences, Curitiba, v.5, n.3, p.01-20, 2024 
animates and brings life to a square. Through the rhythm and sound composition, 
the sonority of the fountain can sometimes be more significant than its visual 
display. Just as with its other aspects—visual, aesthetic, or symbolic—the noise 
and sound of a fountain strongly mark the space and environment in which it is 
located. Sound, in this case, can be an element of marking, disconnection, 
connection, or articulation between spaces that are otherwise difficult to reconcile. 
A concept of marking boundaries and sonic delimitation of space takes shape and 
comes into existence around a sound source in an urban space. 
 
1.3 RESEARCH OBJECTIVES 
 
This paper will mainly focus on the presentation of the methodological 
aspect along with some results and conclusions. 
The main objectives are, first, to study the sound marking and acoustic 
delimitation in urban areas from different perspectives. The study is conducted 
using multiple analysis criteria, allowing for a deeper understanding of this 
phenomenon. Another aim is to closely examine the social and daily use of urban 
space through sound marking and zoning in public spaces. Finally, one of the goals 
of this study is to develop a multidisciplinary approach to address the phenomenon 
of sound delimitation, with the eventual aim of controlling urban noise. 
 
2 METHODOLOGY 
 
In all research hypotheses surrounding the concept of spatial delimitation in 
the perceptual and social practice of public spaces, our approach followed several 
lines of investigation. Each phase aimed to highlight the parameters conditioning 
different aspects of the sound phenomenon and to better grasp its manifestations 
in the environment. Indeed, sound may have effects beyond those classified as 
acoustic or perceptual. The fountain was considered a central noise source, 
around which various phenomena related to sound environments in urban areas 
were shaped and manifested. The investigative stages were as follows: 
1. Acoustic study, metrological measurements, and identifying forms of 
manifestation of the sonic marking and delimitation phenomenon. 
 
 
 
 
8 
Studies in Engineering and Exact Sciences, Curitiba, v.5, n.3, p.01-20, 2024 
2. Psycho-sociological survey to restore the socio-cultural and symbolic 
dimension of the concept of delimitation and sonic marking. This involved 
understanding the space-noise-living experience interrelations around the 
sound source through acoustic and perceptual phenomena. 
3. Sound recording and sequence analysis taken from various sound 
environments. This further reinforced the effort to identify the phenomenon 
of sonic marking and the concept of spatial and territorial delimitation 
already explored through metrology and psycho-sociological surveys. 
 
3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS 
 
3.1 ACOUSTIC STUDY, METROLOGICAL MEASUREMENTS: 
 
3.1.1 Results 
A phase of measurements was carried out to account for various aspects of 
the sound environment in urban sites. Metrology is a necessary, though 
insufficient, tool for studying the practice of urban space through acoustic and 
perceptual components. Other dimensions of noise appear to be important in 
analyzing issues related to the concept or phenomenon of sonic marking in public 
spaces. Metrology can provide a clear understanding of the phenomenon in 
question. 
For acoustic measurements, numerous points were designated at each site 
to conduct this phase of fieldwork. The number and location of these points were 
primarily dictated by the spatial configuration of the area and the nature of the 
acoustic aspect or sound phenomenon to be highlighted. A preliminary study of 
each site helped determine locations aimed at providing a global assessment of 
the sound environment at different times of day. The presence of cascading or jet 
fountains in most of the studied public squares required additional measurement 
points, enabling the spectral analysis of the fountain noise.

3.3 RECORDING AND SOUND SEQUENCE WORK 
 
3.3.1 Results 
 
A phase of sound recordings was carried out simultaneously with the 
metrological surveys at the study sites. The sound recordings and the use of these 
sound sequences added a complementary dimension to the metrological work and 
sociological surveys conducted at each site. 
A sound sequence can be as rich in auditory information as a well-taken 
photograph is in visual detail. Listening to and analyzing these sequences often 
bridges the gap between the subjective expressions from interviews and the 
objective data from physical measurements. Analyzing sound fragments from the 
same point or from different points on the site, in a particular order or another, can 
sometimes more effectively reveal certain sequential aspects and potential 
modulations of the studied sound environment, particularly highlighting the 
existence of sound markings and territorial boundaries in the space. 
To capture such spatial modulations along a given axis of the site, the 
process of sound recording was divided into several static points (measurement 
points), where both recordings and acoustic measurements were taken. In this part 
of the study, sound marking and the perceptual concept of sound delimitation were 
clearly discernible in the treatment of the sound tracks. 
The selection of sound fragments for recording was based on their richness 
and relevance in representing various aspects of sound delimitation and marking 
in the space. These aspects often manifested through fluctuations and masking 
phenomena, as well as interferences among the different noises that make up the

urban sound environment, such as the sound of passing trams, cars, people 
talking, and other sonic layers like the fountain noise, urban drone, and background 
noise. These elements gave rise to a number of clearly delineated acoustic spaces, 
shaped by the different manifestations of sound.

4 CONCLUSIONS 
 
As a result of this study on the aspect of sound marking boundaries and 
delimitation in urban space, it can generally be said that the sound of the fountain 
effectively highlights the spatial dimension of a public square. This constant sound 
marker often provides the sensation of being in an enclosed or open space. The 
perception of the fountain's water sound alone is sometimes enough to convey the 
spatial quality of the place. The presence or absence of sound reflections clearly 
reveals a certain aspect of the spatiality of the location. The sound of an enclosed 
or open space reinforces the visual perception of the spatiality of the place by either 
overlaying or not overlaying the site’s morphological boundaries. 
• Reverberation in such spaces, for example, brings distances closer and 
narrows the space. This is a form of delimitation that differs from the 
morphological boundaries of the space. These aspects are clearly 
highlighted both by interviews and by the measurement of various acoustic 
parameters in the public space. The opposite effect (or absence of 
reverberation) is observed or felt in open urban spaces. 
• The sound of the fountain significantly modulates the perception of space. 
The presence of water noise in the center of a public square creates a 
 
 
 
 
16 
Studies in Engineering and Exact Sciences, Curitiba, v.5, n.3, p.01-20, 2024 
disconnect between the sound or perceptual space and the physical or 
morphological space of the place. The nature and extent of this disconnect 
profoundly affect the quality and experience of the space. This is a form of 
spatial sound delimitation that complements the morphological one, and 
through overlap or layering, it creates a sense of marking boundaries in the 
experience and use of urban space. 
• In terms of designing public spaces for comfort, the sound of a fountain 
could be used to modulate the perception of urban space spatiality and, in 
turn, adjust the boundaries shaped by sound, visual, or morphological 
boundaries. The experience of the urban place depends on this relationship. 
One practical outcome of this study is the idea that the sound of a fountain 
can remedy or mitigate the perception of overly enclosed or overly open 
spaces or correct other shortcomings in the perception and use of urban 
space. 
• In many cases in this study, space appears clearly delimited by sound. 
Around a fountain in a public square, sound boundaries shaped by the 
relationship between water noise and urban noise, based on the classic 
figure-ground schema, were observed. These relationships are, in turn, 
modulated by different acoustic or perceptual phenomena, such as sound 
masking, interference, and reflection. For most people, it is more 
comfortable when the balance is in favor of the water noise. This is clearly 
reflected in the timing, frequency, and spatial extent of daily activities in 
urban spaces or sub-spaces where this balance is achieved. Some spaces 
are more frequented than others depending on the soundscape of the place. 
Thus, a certain delimitation is created, which is clearly evident in how people 
perceive, use, and live in the space. In some of the studied squares, 
benches and café tables were arranged in geometric patterns that clearly 
reflect zoning based on sound data, often forming concentric arrangements 
around the fountain’s noise. 
• The study also noted that many fountains, despite being weaker in sound 
intensity compared to the surrounding urban noise, manage to cover their 
morphological space to some extent. This phenomenon of sound marking 
is evident in almost all the sites studied. This has led to a reconsideration of 
 
 
 
 
17 
Studies in Engineering and Exact Sciences, Curitiba, v.5, n.3, p.01-20, 2024 
the concept of acoustic space in the management of urban areas. An 
acoustic space, whether or not it aligns with the morphological space, is an 
inevitable reality created by any dominant sound source in the urban 
soundscape. This is particularly the case in this study, where the presence 
of a fountain in a public square was examined. While some fountains 
confine their acoustic space within the morphological boundaries of the 
square, others, with higher sound intensity or richer spectral compositions 
in high frequencies, extend beyond these limits. Finally, the study revealed 
that some fountains, during busy times, struggle to be heard just a few 
meters away. 
• The sound delimitation of space depends on several factors, many of which 
were highlighted in this study. These include aspects related to the 
morphology and acoustic qualities (soundscape) of the space, the nature of 
the fountain’s noise (intensity, frequency composition, etc.), and the types 
of organization and composition of the surrounding urban sound 
environment.

• Further studies which aimed at developing operational tools to integrate the 
dimension of noise in the design of public spaces, with the objective of 
establishing a sound boundary for these urban areas. 
• Consider studies that would aim to explore the physical and perceptual 
aspects of the reconfiguration of urban noise by using the sound of fountains 
as an acoustic mask in urban settings. 
• Study of fountains as urban furniture and sound instrumentation of public

松本昌二**・村田俊雄***・宮腰和弘****
Shoji MATSUMOTO, Toshio MURATA and Kazuhiro MIYAKOSHI
1.　はじめに
大規模小売店舗法(大店法)は
、昭和48年の施行以
来厳しく運営されて、その行き過ぎの規制に対して批判
もあった。1990年代に入り、米国が規制緩和を強く要望
するようになり、平成4年(1992)改
正大店法が施行さ
れ、商調協(商業活動調整協議会)が廃止された。商業
調整の場が国と県の大規模小売店舗審議会(大店審議会)
に移り、市民参加は大店審議会主導の意見聴取会議に
なったため、通過儀礼的傾向が強くなった。一方、自動
車保有台数の増加により、買物行動は広域化したため、
郊外大型店の出店が加速することになった。
90年代も中盤を過ぎると、大型店が周辺の地域環境
に及ぼす問題が表面化されてきた。しかし、大店法は交
通渋滞、駐車場の容量確保、騒音、ゴミ等の問題に対し
て無力であった。また大店法の需給調整が、これらの問
題の顕在化で時代にそぐわなくなってきたともいえる。
米国は再三にわたって大店法の廃止を要求してきた。
そのような流れの変化を受け、1997年末政府は大店法
の廃止を決定し、大型店の出店を環境面から規制する大
規模小売店舗立地法(大店立地法)が2000年6月
から施
行された。
さて、大店法の需給調整の下で出店した大型店につい
て、周辺環境の影響評価を行った研究事例は少ない。ま
た大型店周辺地域の居住者の意見を調査した事例も少な
い。1)2)
そこで本研究では、新潟県をケーススタディとして、
大店法による需給調整、大型店の立地、それによる商圏
や地域構造の変化等を分析する。次に、大型店の駐車場
や交通実態、周辺環境影響の評価、周辺住民の意識を把
握する。大店法のもとで出店した大型店について、需給
調整、環境影響、地域づくりの3視
点からアプローチす
ることにより、大店立地法とその運用にかかわる課題を
検討することを目的とする。
2.　研究の視点と方法
大店法の目的は、消費者の保護と周辺小売業の事業活
動の保護であり、出店する店舗は、規模により第一種・
第二種に分けられ需給調整が行われてきた。規制の対象
となるのは、店舗の開店日、店舗面積、閉店時刻、年間
休業日数であり、「調整4項目」とよばれる。また出店時
は、(1)開発許可申請・出店計画概要書の作成、(2)所轄警
察署や道路管理者との出入口や安全性に関した協議、(3)
大規模小売店舗審議会(意見聴取会議を開催する)、(4)建
築申請といった手順を経て店舗の建築に至る。
一方、大店立地法の目的は、その周辺地域の生活環境
の保持のため大型店施設の配置と運営方法の適正化であ

5.　おわりに
以上、大店法の下での分析結果を要約する。
(1)県内市町村の多くで、1990年代に大型店の出店が一
段と活発になり、小売業の大型店占有率が極めて高
くなったが、それは国の規制緩和に沿った大店法の
運用、需給調整の結果である。
(2)新規の大型店出店の多くは郊外立地であり、商圏は
市町村域を越えて拡大した。中心地区(中心商店街)
側はその変化に対応できなかったため、中心市街地
の空洞化、衰退を招いた。
(3)大型店周辺環境の実態調査によれば、周辺住民には
不満の意見が多いこと、大型店からの廃棄物や騒音
といった実害は少なく、むしろアクセス道路の交通
騒音や渋滞といった交通環境が問題であることがわ
かった。
(4)大型店周辺の住環境評価によれば、静けさが悪化した、
自動車交通量が増えたという反面、買い物の利便性は
向上したと、各店舗に共通した評価結果が得られた。
(5)上述の実態調査結果、及び大規模小売店舗審議会にお
いても大型店の周辺環境を懸念する意見が多く出され
たことから、大型店の出店に際して周辺環境アセスメ
ントを行うことは十分な妥当性を持っている。
以上の実態に基づいて、2000年6月から施行された大
店立地法とその運用について、今後の問題点を検討する。
まず、既に述べたように、大店立地法は周辺地域の生
活環境を保持するため大型店施設の配置と運営方法の適
正化を目的としている。その第4条
の「指針」である大
型店設置者が配慮すべき事項を検討すると、環境をアセ
スメントする手法にいくつか問題点があると言えよう。
従って、大店立地法とその運用に関して、本研究の結果
から判断して、少なくとも以下の問題点を指摘すること
ができる。
(1)大店立地法の施行下においても、道路管理者、公安委
員会等による交通協議は従来通り行政指導で行なわれる。
新潟県の場合でいえば、県庁内連絡協議会と大規模小売
立地審議会(大店立地審議会)において交通協議の内容
が公開され調整されることになる。従来よりは情報公開、
透明性が改善されるが、周辺住民の意見や理解を求める
仕組みや努力が必要とされる。
(2)大店立地法4条
の「指針」によれば、予測・評価の対
象となる騒音は駐車場からの騒音、敷地内における自動
車走行等による騒音などであり、大型店へのアクセス道
路における自動車走行の騒音は含まれていない。本研究
によれば、アクセス道路の騒音こそが問題であり、それ
に対応する仕組みが必要となる。
(3)大店立地法4条
の「指針」では、必要駐車場台数の計
算式を提示している。本研究では、既存店舗の駐車場台
数をこの計算式と比較することはしていない。同計算式
―185―
 の算定根拠は不明確であるため、新潟県の実状に合うの
か、業種によってどう違うのか、検討が必要である。
(4)大店立地法において住民参加にかかわることは、地元
説明会、地元住民・地元商工会議所の意見(縦覧)、及
び新潟県では大店立地審議会である。直接の利害関係を
持つ大型店周辺、近隣の商業者や住民の意見が強く反映
されることが懸念されるため、より広域的に住民の意見
を聞く機会を設けることが望まれる。アセスメントへの
住民参加を推進するために、地域独自のルールづくりが
必要である。
次に、「大店立地法第4条
の指針(案)の策定に当たっ
て」によれば、地域づくり・街づくりは総合的な取り組
みであり、公共投資、助成策、規制など多様なツールが
組み合わされることが必要であると認め、大型店立地の
適否の判断に直接かかわるものについては、都市計画法
等のいわゆるゾーニング的手法(特別用途地区の設定の
弾力化を含む)を活用することが不可欠であるとしてい
る。実際「まちづくり3法」の一つの柱として、平成10
年5月
、都市計画法が改正されて、特別用途地域の指定
は市町村に権限を与え、市町村が設定できるようになっ
た。しかし、市町村が中小小売店舗地区等の特別用途地
区を現実に指定できるかとなると極めて困難と思われ、
まして都市計画区域外の町村にとっては役に立たないこ
とになると指摘されてきた。
大型店出店において地域づくり・街づくりの第3の
視
点の重要性を認めつつも、国はそのために十分な対策を
とらないまま、大店立地法をスタートさせたと言えよ
う。地域づくり・街づくりの視点から、地域の実情に
あった、きめ細かい対応をするために、地域独自のルー
ル「街づくり条例」が必要であるという意見が多い。9)
それはもちろん大型店、小売業に限定されたものとは限
らないが、地域の取り組みが高まることを期待したい。

Location of Large-Scale 
Retail Stores under Economic Regulation and their Impact Assessment 
on Neighbor-
hood Environment-A 
Case Study in Niigata Prefecture-
Shoji MATSUMOTO, Toshio MURATA and Kazuhiro MIYAKOSHI
The law of large-scale retail stores (LSs) had economically regulated their new location and operation until 2000, and a com-
pletely new law for the location of LSs came into effect from June 2000. The study analyzes the location ofLSs and consumers'
shopping behavior during 1990's in Niigata Prefecture, and assesses traffic and living environment around some of LSs as a case
study. The number of newly opened LSs grew very high during 1990's throughout Niigata Prefecture. Vitality of central shopping
areas declined in most of cities. Inhabitants around the LSs revealed a unsatisfactory opinion mostly towards traffic jams of access
route and traffic noise, particularly during operating hours late at night. The paper discusses several enforcement and operation
issues to be faced by the new location law which are to environmentally assess impacts of the location of LSs.
