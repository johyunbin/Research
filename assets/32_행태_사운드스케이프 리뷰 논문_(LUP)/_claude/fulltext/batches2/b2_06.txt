A B S T R A C T
Urban characteristics can shape physical activity, but evidence regarding their joint role alongside individual 
perceptions remains limited. This study examined the combined associations of multiple environmental char­
acteristics with physical activity across the five most populous cities in Bulgaria. A cross-sectional survey of 4640 
adults was conducted in 2023. Physical activity across different intensity levels was measured with the short 
form of the International Physical Activity Questionnaire and linked to objective measures of neighborhood 
walkability, fine particulate matter, road traffic noise, area-level socioeconomic status (SES), and availability of 
greenspace and bluespace within 300 m and 1000 m buffers. Presence of a home garden was self-reported, along 
with perceived walkability, bikeability, greenspace, bluespace, and sociodemographic factors. Results showed 
that walkability, greenspace, area-level SES, home gardens, perceived bluespace, high/low perceived greenspace 
(vs. medium), and bikeability were positively associated with time spent in vigorous physical activity. Walk­
ability, greenspace, perceived bluespace, low/high perceived greenspace, home gardens, and bluespace were 
positively associated with moderate-to-vigorous physical activity. Objective and perceived walkability, medium/ 
high levels of greenspace, low/high area-level SES, and home gardens were positively associated with walking. 
Associations remained robust after accounting for air pollution and noise, which were unexpectedly positively 
associated with certain forms of physical activity. These findings appear meaningful from a preventive 
perspective and may provide arguments for improving urban design conducive to physical activity and urban 
planning initiatives.

Methods
2.1. Survey design and sampling
Between August and October 2023, a representative quota sample of 
the general population was collected from the five most populous cities 
in Bulgaria. The study areas included the capital, Sofia (~1.2 million 
inhabitants), as well as Plovdiv (~330 000), Varna (~320 000), Burgas 
(~190 000), and Ruse (~120 000). Sofia, Plovdiv, and Ruse are land­
locked with varying access to rivers (e.g., Iskar, Maritsa, and the Dan­
ube), whereas Varna and Burgas are located on the Black Sea coast. A 
map of the study areas is shown in Fig. S1 in Supplementary File 1.
A detailed description of the Bulgarian Healthy Urban Environment 
study is provided elsewhere (Dzhambov et al., 2025). Briefly, partici­
pants were approached at their homes by trained interviewers from a 
professional survey company. Bulgarian-speaking adults aged ≥18 years 
residing at their current address for at least one year were included. 
Participants were sampled according to quotas from predefined spatial 
typologies within each city to improve spatial contrast and thus, in­
crease analytical power. A total of 4640 participants were included, with 
response rates ranging from 30% in Sofia to 58% in Plovdiv.
Participants provided informed consent verbally. All procedures 
followed relevant EU data protection regulations. The study was 
approved by the Scientific Ethics Committee at the Medical University of 
Plovdiv (Protocol No. 4/May 04, 2023; Opinion No. P-1253/May 17, 
2023).
2.2. Physical activity
The International Physical Activity Questionnaire-Short Form 
(IPAQ-SF) was used to assess participants’ physical activity across 
different intensity levels (Craig et al., 2003). Participants were instruc­
ted to recall a typical most recent week, during which they were neither 
sick nor travelling out of the city. Vigorous physical activity (VPA) was 
defined as activity performed for at least 10 min at a time that required 
substantial physical effort and resulted in breathing much harder than 
normal (e.g., lifting heavy weights, fast running, or fast cycling). 
Moderate-intensity physical activity (MPA) was defined as activity 
lasting at least 10 min that required moderate physical effort and caused

cycling at a normal pace). Walking was defined as any walking for at 
least 10 min at a time, including walking at work or at home, for 
transportation, during leisure time, or while engaging in sports.
Data processing involved converting all reported activity durations 
to minutes and excluding implausible values in accordance with IPAQ 
scoring guidelines (IPAQ Research Committee). Participants reporting 
total physical activity that exceeded 960 min per day were excluded. 
Reported activity durations of less than 10 min were recoded to 0 min 
because very short bouts of physical activity are less likely to activate 
metabolic pathways, and durations greater than 180 min were truncated 
to 180 min because they were considered unlikely.
Outcome variables were constructed from self-reported frequency 
(days per week) and duration (minutes per day) of physical activity. 
Compliance with established physical activity guidelines (WHO, 2020) 
was operationalized with dichotomous indicators: ≥75 min per week of 
VPA, and ≥150 min per week of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity 
(MPA + 2*VPA = MVPA), and walking for ≥150 min per week. MVPA 
was calculated as the sum of MPA plus twice the time spent in VPA, 
consistent with the WHO equivalence assumption (WHO, 2020) and 
IPAQ MET-based scoring (IPAQ Research Committee). Additionally, 
continuous measures of VPA, MPA, and walking time were computed in 
minutes per week among participants reporting non-zero activity levels.
2.3. Environmental characteristics
Participants’ residential environment was characterized using both 
objective measures and self-reported perceptions, as these provide 
complementary information. The literature and data availability 
informed the choice of physical environmental characteristics (Smith 
et al., 2017).
2.3.1. Objective neighborhood characteristics
Walkability refers to the extent to which the built environment en­
courages walking (Gao et al., 2020). It was calculated using 2021 census 
population density data, Shannon's index for land use diversity derived 
from 2018 European Urban Atlas (Copernicus Land Monitoring Service, 
2021), land use/land cover and cadastral data (GCCA, 2016), and 
OpenStreetMap-derived (OpenStreetMap contributors, 2023) four-way 
intersection density (Dzhambov et al., 2025). The z-scores of these 
three input variables were summarized within circular buffers of 300 m 
and 1000 m. These buffer sizes correspond to typical walking distances 
near one's home, considering the size of the study areas, and fall within a 
range deemed relevant to physical activity (Browning and Lee, 2017; 
Wang et al., 2021; Zare Sakhvidi et al., 2025).

A B S T R A C T
Autonomous electric vehicles (AEVs) will increasingly characterize future motorized traffic in 
urban areas. These vehicles are often silent and can be driverless. Therefore, understanding the 
interactions between these vehicles and vulnerable road users (VRUs) is crucial for traffic safety. 
This study explores the impact of an additional auditory alert system and external human- 
machine interfaces (eHMIs) on AEVs on cyclists' interaction behavior, comfort, and safety 
under varying vehicle–signal conditions and environmental noise levels. This study employed 
immersive Virtual Reality (VR) technology to simulate cycling across 18 traffic scenarios, using a 
within-subjects design with 40 participants. These scenarios included two vehicle types: auton­
omous conventional vehicles (ACVs) and autonomous electric vehicles (AEVs), two levels of 
ambient noise levels (quiet residential areas; noisier streets), and three levels of eHMI signal 
trigger distances (15, 20, 25 m). Data regarding cycling behavior (e.g., response distance, speed 
variation, gaze direction) and cyclists' perceptions (e.g., realism, simulator sickness, presence, 
trust, and perceived safety) were collected through VR and post-experiment questionnaires, 
respectively. We found that compared to vehicles equipped only with an eHMI, AEVs with 
auditory alerts significantly enhanced cyclists' perceptions and responses, with cyclists detecting 
the vehicles earlier and responding faster. Subjective feedback also indicated a stronger sense of 
safety and comfort, such as smoother deceleration that indicated natural responses and reduced 
stress. Participants also reported a more comfortable interaction experience with AEVs with 
auditory alerts. These findings provide evidence that equipping AEVs with additional auditory 
alerts improves the safety and comfort of cyclists in their interactions with AEVs in urban envi­
ronments. Therefore, policymakers are advised to consider this when setting regulations for the 
deployment of AEVs in urban areas.

Methodology
2.1. Experiment design
This study employed immersive VR experiments to investigate the effects of environmental noise level, vehicle–signal condition, 
and eHMI signal trigger distance on cyclists' behavior. A within-subject design approach was adopted to eliminate individual differ­
ences for comparison across different scenarios. The main variables that were considered in the experiment and their definitions, 
including independent variables, intermediate variables, and dependent variables, are presented in Table 1.
This study focused on three primary independent variables and their effects on cyclists' behavior, namely environmental noise 
level, vehicle–signal condition (a categorical variable with three levels), and eHMI signal trigger distance. These variables were 
designed to simulate various conditions that cyclists may encounter in real traffic environments and to analyze their impact on cyclists' 
behavioral and perceptual responses. This study systematically manipulated these three independent variables, generating a total of 
eighteen experimental scenarios. These scenarios were evenly divided into two levels of environmental noise, representing quieter and 
noisier ambient sound conditions within the same roadside street context. Within each level, the scenarios were further subdivided into 
nine distinct conditions determined by variations in the eHMI signal trigger distance (i.e., 15 m, 20 m, 25 m) and vehicle–signal 
condition (i.e., ACV with visual eHMI, AEV with visual eHMI, and AEV with visual eHMI and additional auditory alert).

simulator. The AASHTO Guide for the Development of Bicycle Facilities assumes a bicyclist perception–reaction time of 2.5 s when 
determining minimum stopping sight distance (American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials (AASHTO), 
1999). In the pilot sessions, participants typically cycled at approximately 5 m/s in the simulator. On this basis, 15 m was selected 
as the minimum trigger distance, corresponding to approximately 3 s of advance warning, which is slightly above the 2.5-s reference

Abstract
Rural public spaces are crucial to the daily activities of older adults; however, limited
research has examined the effects of their environmental characteristics on older adults’
spatiotemporal behavior and perception from a multisensory perspective. This study
hypothesizes that composite sensory environments have significant nonlinear predictive
effects on older adults’ behavior types and satisfaction. In this study, 10 sample spaces
were selected in Qingdao, China. Multi-source data were collected through a two-week
period of unobtrusive observation and subjective questionnaire surveys (N = 241). Multiple
logistic regression was used to analyze the main effects of environmental characteristics,
and an MLP model with a single hidden layer of 100 units was constructed to predict dwell
time and satisfaction. The results show that, in the investigated rural context, older adults’
dominant behavior was social activity (81.12%), which mainly occurred in built spaces such
as squares. Multiple logistic regression indicated that, among the various environmental
factors, visual aesthetics had a statistically significant effect on behavior types (p = 0.013).
The MLP model achieved prediction accuracies of 85.3% for dwell time and 93.1% for
satisfaction. The key predictive variables were volume perception (100% importance), the
Natural Sound Index (NSI) (92.1%), and visual aesthetics (89.3%). Subgroup heterogeneity
analysis further showed that older-old adults and those with poorer health conditions
were more sensitive to pavement quality and physical comfort, whereas older adults living
alone or with limited household companionship were more strongly influenced by visual
aesthetics and natural soundscape quality. The theoretical significance of this study lies in
proposing quantitative measures of natural sound and odor indices and revealing that, in
the specific northern rural built environment, the coordinated design of visual and auditory
environments plays an important role in improving spatial quality. The findings provide
empirical support for the age-friendly micro-renewal of rural public spaces in specific
regions. However, due to the limitations of single-season data and a relatively small sample
size, their generalizability needs to be further verified across regions.

1. Introduction
The intensification of rapid urbanization and population mobility has led rural China
to face more severe aging challenges than urban areas [1]. Data from the Seventh National
Population Census show that the proportion of people aged 60 years and above in ru-
ral China has reached 23.81%, which is significantly higher than that in urban areas [2].
Against this background, rural public spaces serve as core settings for older adults’ daily
social interaction, emotional attachment, and physical exercise. Their age-friendly envi-
ronmental quality is directly related to the health and well-being of rural residents and
the quality of aging in place [3,4]. However, due to long-standing “urban-biased” poli-
cies, the theory and practice of age-friendly public spaces have mainly focused on urban
communities [5,6], while the micro-environmental construction of rural public spaces has
often been marginalized [7–9].
In recent years, some scholars have begun to focus on age-friendliness in rural spaces,
but existing studies still show clear dimensional limitations [3]. A large body of literature
mainly focuses on the construction of older adults’ life circles at the macro level, the
spatial allocation of elderly care service facilities, or neighborhood network support from a
sociological perspective [8,9]. Although these studies emphasize the macro-level necessity
of rural age-friendliness, most of them overlook the specific mechanisms through which
the physical characteristics of the micro-scale built environment influence older adults’
high-frequency daily spatiotemporal behavior [10]. Rural society is essentially a typical
“acquaintance society”, and the outdoor activities of older adults often arise from the
need for functional gathering [7]. Therefore, analyzing the mapping relationship between
objective physical environmental characteristics, older adults’ spatiotemporal behavior,
and subjective perception at the micro-spatial scale remains an important blind spot that
urgently needs to be addressed in current rural living environment research [11,12].

on information received through a single dimension, but is a composite process formed
by the coordinated action of multiple sensory channels, including vision, hearing, smell,
and touch [13,14]. Environmental stimuli, as independent variables, trigger different

--- METHODS/RESULTS EXCERPT ---
Methods
2.1. Case Study Area
This study selected two typical rural villages, Xiaolingzi Village and Xishiling Village,
in the West Coast New Area of Qingdao, China, as the study area (Figure 1). The aging
rate in rural Qingdao has reached 20.28% [23]. Due to the traditional concept of “aging
in place”, older adults in these areas have a very low willingness to migrate, making the
study area highly typical and representative [24].
In terms of sample selection, five public spaces were selected in each village, resulting
in a total of 10 sample spaces. These included spaces under trees, squares, areas in front of
service centers, roadways, and spaces surrounding houses. The selection criteria were as
follows: (1) full public accessibility; and (2) the presence of regular daily activities by older
adults. Although the number of spatial samples (N = 10) was relatively small in terms of ge-
ographic scale, this study adopted high-frequency and long-term behavioral tracking over
14 consecutive days, with observations conducted for 14 h per day. Dense time-series data
were used to compensate for the limited number of spatial samples, thereby ensuring that

A B S T R A C T
Introduction: Built environment attributes are widely recognized as key levers for promoting 
cycling. However, their implementation is often limited by the spatial and economic constraints 
of large-scale urban redevelopment. Existing research has largely overlooked the nonlinear and 
potentially threshold-based relationships between environmental features and cycling behavior. 
Moreover, cyclists’ sensory perceptions of street-level audiovisual environments remain insuffi­
ciently considered, despite their importance for environmental comfort and perceived safety.
Method: This study integrates interpretable machine learning and computer vision techniques to 
examine how the perceived audiovisual environment influences cycling volume at the street level. 
Using large-scale crowdsourced cycling trajectory data from a fitness tracking application and 
street-level imagery, we derived audiovisual and built-environment indicators to train an 
XGBoost model. Partial dependence analysis was employed to visualize and interpret the 
nonlinear effects of key environmental features.
Results: The findings reveal that both built and audiovisual factors exhibit complex nonlinear 
associations with cycling volume, characterized by variable-specific thresholds and saturation 
effects. Streets with greater visual openness, stronger presence of natural sounds, lower noise 
intensity, and higher perceived safety were associated with substantially higher cycling volumes. 
Acoustic attributes, particularly natural and mechanical sound components, showed distinct 
nonlinear responses.
Conclusion: This study provides new empirical evidence that street-level sensory environments 
significantly influence cycling behavior in nonlinear ways. The results indicate that relatively 
low-cost design interventions, including enhancing visual openness, using vegetation to reduce 
noise, and strengthening perceived safety, can support the retrofitting of existing streetscapes. 
These insights can support planners and policymakers in advancing more inclusive and sustain­
able urban mobility.

Methods
3.1. Study area
In this study, the central urban area of Shanghai, China, was selected as the case study site (Fig. 1). As one of China's highest-density 
cities, Shanghai has in recent years strongly promoted green mobility and the development of non-motorized transport systems, with 
continued improvements to cycling infrastructure. Under the planning framework of the “15-min community life circle,” cycling has 
been repositioned as a key mode of sustainable urban mobility. The rapid expansion of the bikeshare system and the implementation of 
cycling-friendly street retrofitting projects have contributed to a noticeable resurgence in cycling, with an increasing share of daily 
trips now made by bicycle. The city's flat topography and well-connected road network further support short-distance urban cycling. In 
addition, recent policies promoting low-carbon transport and sustainable development provide a policy-driven context for studying 
cycling behavior. Together, these factors make Shanghai a compelling case for examining how street-level audiovisual environments 
influence cycling activity in dense urban settings.
3.2. Analytical framework
This study proposes an analytical framework to assess the influence of street environments on cycling, as illustrated in Fig. 2. The 
framework consists of four distinct steps aimed at enhancing our understanding of the environmental relationships between audio­
visual characteristics of urban streets and cycling volume. First, Baidu Street View imagery, Points of Interest (POIs), and Open­
StreetMap (OSM) road network data were collected and processed (Section 3.3). Built environment (BE) features were derived by 
integrating POI distributions and street network metrics within a GIS-based framework. Second, cycling volume data were obtained at 
the street segment level using cycling trajectory records from the KEEP platform (Section 3.4). Third, street-level audiovisual features 
were quantified using machine learning and street view imagery (Section 3.5). These features included streetscape elements, 
soundscape characteristics, and perceived environmental qualities. Specifically, streetscape refers to the objective visual character­
istics of the street, while soundscape and perceived quality represent the predicted acoustic environment (Zhao et al., 2023) and 
subjective impressions (Gao and Fang, 2025c; Rui and Xu, 2024), respectively, derived from computer vision-based assessments. 
Finally, an XGBoost model was employed to capture the nonlinear relationships between environmental features and cycling volume.

3.3. Data collection
Street view imagery (SVI) was obtained through the Baidu Maps Application Programming Interface (API), which provides access 
to an extensive repository of panoramic images captured across China. To ensure spatial coverage and adequately represent the visual 
characteristics of the study area, street-level imagery was sampled at 100-m intervals along all streets within the defined urban 
boundary. A total of 24,529 sampling points were included, and image acquisition was completed by the end of 2024. Although 
seasonal variation in vegetation exists in Shanghai, the majority of dominant street trees are evergreen species, and the spatial 
configuration of greenery remains relatively stable across seasons. Therefore, the captured imagery primarily reflects structural 
streetscape characteristics rather than short-term phenological fluctuations. Baidu's street view system typically captures imagery 
under clear and temperate weather conditions, ensuring relatively consistent visual quality. Following established practices from 
previous studies, API parameters were specified as follows: image resolution was set to 1024 × 1024 pixels, and four images were 
retrieved from each sampling point at horizontal field-of-view angles of 0◦, 90◦, 180◦, and 270◦, respectively. This approach enabled a 
comprehensive panoramic representation of each streetscape. The resulting imagery served as the basis for estimating street-level 
audiovisual environments using machine learning and computer vision techniques (see Sections 3.5.1, 3.5.2, and 3.5.3).
3.4. Dependent variable
Cycling GPS trajectory data were obtained from the KEEP fitness application, one of the most widely used sports tracking apps in 
China and comparable to Strava in the United States. KEEP has more than 300 million registered users, including office workers, and 
cycling enthusiasts (Li et al., 2025). The trajectory data used in this study were collected from users who recorded and uploaded their 
cycling activities to the KEEP platform during July 2023. The platform generates structured volunteered geographic information (VGI), 
including detailed ride-level information such as trajectory ID, trip duration, distance, and start and end times. KEEP-based trajectory 
data have been increasingly applied across various research fields, including trajectory pattern mining (Li et al., 2025), the relationship 
between the built environment and active travel (Zhang, S. et al., 2024), and exercise intensity analysis (Qiu et al., 2024), underscoring 
their growing value and potential for urban cycling behavior research. Trips recorded on KEEP are more likely to reflect exercise,
