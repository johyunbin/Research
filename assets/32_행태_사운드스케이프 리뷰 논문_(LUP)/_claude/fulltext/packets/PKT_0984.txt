ABSTRACT 
The health of the urban public can be promoted by shaping ecological 
landscapes and reducing noise. Visual landscapes and soundscape, con­
sidered as important components of sustainable urban development, 
are becoming increasingly concerned. In this study, Hefei Feicui Lake 
scenic spot in China was regarded as an example. By using question­
naire survey, soundwalks and acoustic monitoring methods, which 
aimed to understand people’s sense of identification with musical 
theme landscape and soundscape in the area, and further explored the 
effects of the interaction between musical theme landscape and sound­
scape on the perceived soundscape restoration of visitors. The results 
showed that musical theme landscape and soundscape could affect 
people’s emotions on the individual level and their behaviours, such as 
staying (viewing the scenery or rest) on the social level. On this basis, a 
strategy for creating urban public space inspired by the art of music 
was proposed as the topic of this study.

Introduction
From a visual perspective, the impact of urban landscapes on human psychology is gradually 
receiving attention (Deng et al., 2020; Jeon & Jo, 2020; Tan, Hasegawa, Lau, & Tang, 2022). On 
the other hand, noise has a negative impact on people’s daily lives from an auditory perspective, 
and many countries are emphasising the negative impact of noise on public health (Wang, He, 
Li, & Xiao, 2023). Therefore, the urban landscape and acoustic environment, regarded as impor­
tant environmental elements in creating the urban overall space, gradually gained increasing 
attention and have been studied thoroughly (Deng et al., 2020; Jeon & Jo, 2020; Tan et al., 2022; 
Wang, He, Li, et al., 2023). Although humans shaped landscapes that decorated the environment 
or eliminated these ‘noise’ in urban spaces, a single type of landscape or soundless city is impos­
sible to live in and also unacceptable since other possibilities exist. From another perspective, 
the landscape and the sound of a city are reflections of people’s public life, playing a complex 
role in the city (Deng et al., 2020; Wang, He, Li, et al., 2023; Wang, He, Xiao, 2023).
In the academic community, more and more scholars are realising that the study of landscape 
and sound relied on the physical measurements, especially the study of vision and sound levels 
(Deng et al., 2020; Jeon & Jo, 2020; Kang, 2023; Tan et al., 2022). The urban landscape includes 
natural and artificial landscape elements. The elements of natural landscape mainly refer to the 
natural landscape, such as mountains, ancient trees, stones, rivers, lakes, and oceans. The main

necessary to engage healthy residents in social activities that are even more important for the 
residents with psychological stress. However, the functional research on the soundscape men­
tioned above was only limited to the evaluation of visual or auditory functions.

Feicui scenic spot (11715’ N, 31.86’ E) is located at No. 1 Rongcheng Road, Economic and

Reservoir (Wang, He, Xiao, 2023). The terrain is open and gentle, and the water surface and vari­
ous trees combine to create a beautiful natural scenery in the spot. Feicui Lake scenic spot is div­
ided into four districts and one park, ecological leisure and entertainment scenic spots, dam 
landscape interaction area, Feicui Lake Welcome Hotel Area, and Feicui Park (Wang, He, Li, et al., 
2023). As an ecological scenic area, it mainly consisted of the green space and water surface

The main areas of this study were marked in Figure 7. The Feicui Lake scenic spot adopts vari­
ous forms of musical theme landscape construction, and the violin island (Figure 7(b)), located in

are curved, and the entire body is like a gourd with symmetrical, uniform and gradual characteris­
tics. This shape is close to the free curve advocated by modern gardens. There is also a musical 
fountain in the lake (Figure 7(a)), accompanied by the sound of music, which can dance in differ­
ent forms in night, to achieve a perfect combination of fountain water type, lighting and colour

were investigated by determining on-site sounds through sounwalks, which involved in exploring 
an area through walking, listening to and recording all sounds heard, reflecting on their social and 
cultural background and significance, improving people’s perception of sound, and the perceptual 
relationship between people and the environment. This provides a method for studying the cul­
tural, political, and social significance of urban soundscapes (Xie, Zhang, Lee, Lim, & Lee, 2021). 
Meanwhile, targeted surveys and questionnaire surveys were conducted to provide certain design 
strategies for creating musical theme landscapes in the urban public space.
Questionnaire
This study adopted a combination of soundscape walking and landscape satisfaction question­
naire survey. Soundwalks is commonly used in environmental acoustics research, allowing partici­
pants to carefully listen to the soundscape environment and the sounds they have experienced 
and make judgments. In order to study the acoustic environment of the Feicui Lake scenic spot, 
a soundwalks with 10 sampling points (break locations), at the main scenic spots of Feicui Lake 
was shown in Figure 8. During the research process, 273 Feicui Lake scenic spot tourists were 
randomly interviewed using the Likert scale. This study was conducted in March 2023 in Hefei 
Feicui Lake scenic spot. BSWA801 (Beijing Tongde Entrepreneurship Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, 
China) sound level metres, the Norsonic Nor121 (Norsonic Co., Ltd., Tranby, Norway) and 
HUAWEI nova 3i Mobile (Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China) were used for record­
ing and filming, respectively.
Based on the environmental characteristics of different locations, an evaluation of the corre­
sponding urban space and soundscape design was obtained. After detailed measurements, the 
average sound pressure level (LAeq) was determined on the pedestrian walkway of the violin island 
in the central area of Feicui Lake scenic spot, and BSWA801 sound level metre (Beijing Tongde 
Entrepreneurship Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) was used on a tripod 1.5 metres above the 
ground. The SPL was measured using the Norsonic Nor121 (Norsonic Co., Ltd., Tranby, Norway), and 
then analysed using 01 dB software (01dBMetravib Co., Ltd., Paris, France). During the measurement 
process, the temperature was 20 C and the relative humidity of the research area was 60%. The 
measurements were made according to the ISO sound level standards prepared by the 
International Organisation for Standardisation (ISO 12913-1:2014 Acoustics—Soundscape—Part 1: 
Definition and Conceptual Framework). Three project personnels were conducted simultaneously by 
three graduate students with research experience.
The questionnaire survey was conducted voluntarily by the respondents and obtained written 
informed consent from all study participants. Before filling in the questionnaire, it was confirmed 
that the respondents’ hearing was normal through questioning. The questionnaires were issued
