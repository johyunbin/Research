A B S T R A C T
Introduction: Built environment attributes are widely recognized as key levers for promoting 
cycling. However, their implementation is often limited by the spatial and economic constraints 
of large-scale urban redevelopment. Existing research has largely overlooked the nonlinear and 
potentially threshold-based relationships between environmental features and cycling behavior. 
Moreover, cyclists’ sensory perceptions of street-level audiovisual environments remain insuffi­
ciently considered, despite their importance for environmental comfort and perceived safety.
Method: This study integrates interpretable machine learning and computer vision techniques to 
examine how the perceived audiovisual environment influences cycling volume at the street level. 
Using large-scale crowdsourced cycling trajectory data from a fitness tracking application and 
street-level imagery, we derived audiovisual and built-environment indicators to train an 
XGBoost model. Partial dependence analysis was employed to visualize and interpret the 
nonlinear effects of key environmental features.
Results: The findings reveal that both built and audiovisual factors exhibit complex nonlinear 
associations with cycling volume, characterized by variable-specific thresholds and saturation 
effects. Streets with greater visual openness, stronger presence of natural sounds, lower noise 
intensity, and higher perceived safety were associated with substantially higher cycling volumes. 
Acoustic attributes, particularly natural and mechanical sound components, showed distinct 
nonlinear responses.
Conclusion: This study provides new empirical evidence that street-level sensory environments 
significantly influence cycling behavior in nonlinear ways. The results indicate that relatively 
low-cost design interventions, including enhancing visual openness, using vegetation to reduce 
noise, and strengthening perceived safety, can support the retrofitting of existing streetscapes. 
These insights can support planners and policymakers in advancing more inclusive and sustain­
able urban mobility.

Methods
3.1. Study area
In this study, the central urban area of Shanghai, China, was selected as the case study site (Fig. 1). As one of China's highest-density 
cities, Shanghai has in recent years strongly promoted green mobility and the development of non-motorized transport systems, with 
continued improvements to cycling infrastructure. Under the planning framework of the “15-min community life circle,” cycling has 
been repositioned as a key mode of sustainable urban mobility. The rapid expansion of the bikeshare system and the implementation of 
cycling-friendly street retrofitting projects have contributed to a noticeable resurgence in cycling, with an increasing share of daily 
trips now made by bicycle. The city's flat topography and well-connected road network further support short-distance urban cycling. In 
addition, recent policies promoting low-carbon transport and sustainable development provide a policy-driven context for studying 
cycling behavior. Together, these factors make Shanghai a compelling case for examining how street-level audiovisual environments 
influence cycling activity in dense urban settings.
3.2. Analytical framework
This study proposes an analytical framework to assess the influence of street environments on cycling, as illustrated in Fig. 2. The 
framework consists of four distinct steps aimed at enhancing our understanding of the environmental relationships between audio­
visual characteristics of urban streets and cycling volume. First, Baidu Street View imagery, Points of Interest (POIs), and Open­
StreetMap (OSM) road network data were collected and processed (Section 3.3). Built environment (BE) features were derived by 
integrating POI distributions and street network metrics within a GIS-based framework. Second, cycling volume data were obtained at 
the street segment level using cycling trajectory records from the KEEP platform (Section 3.4). Third, street-level audiovisual features 
were quantified using machine learning and street view imagery (Section 3.5). These features included streetscape elements, 
soundscape characteristics, and perceived environmental qualities. Specifically, streetscape refers to the objective visual character­
istics of the street, while soundscape and perceived quality represent the predicted acoustic environment (Zhao et al., 2023) and 
subjective impressions (Gao and Fang, 2025c; Rui and Xu, 2024), respectively, derived from computer vision-based assessments. 
Finally, an XGBoost model was employed to capture the nonlinear relationships between environmental features and cycling volume.

3.3. Data collection
Street view imagery (SVI) was obtained through the Baidu Maps Application Programming Interface (API), which provides access 
to an extensive repository of panoramic images captured across China. To ensure spatial coverage and adequately represent the visual 
characteristics of the study area, street-level imagery was sampled at 100-m intervals along all streets within the defined urban 
boundary. A total of 24,529 sampling points were included, and image acquisition was completed by the end of 2024. Although 
seasonal variation in vegetation exists in Shanghai, the majority of dominant street trees are evergreen species, and the spatial 
configuration of greenery remains relatively stable across seasons. Therefore, the captured imagery primarily reflects structural 
streetscape characteristics rather than short-term phenological fluctuations. Baidu's street view system typically captures imagery 
under clear and temperate weather conditions, ensuring relatively consistent visual quality. Following established practices from 
previous studies, API parameters were specified as follows: image resolution was set to 1024 × 1024 pixels, and four images were 
retrieved from each sampling point at horizontal field-of-view angles of 0◦, 90◦, 180◦, and 270◦, respectively. This approach enabled a 
comprehensive panoramic representation of each streetscape. The resulting imagery served as the basis for estimating street-level 
audiovisual environments using machine learning and computer vision techniques (see Sections 3.5.1, 3.5.2, and 3.5.3).
3.4. Dependent variable
Cycling GPS trajectory data were obtained from the KEEP fitness application, one of the most widely used sports tracking apps in 
China and comparable to Strava in the United States. KEEP has more than 300 million registered users, including office workers, and 
cycling enthusiasts (Li et al., 2025). The trajectory data used in this study were collected from users who recorded and uploaded their 
cycling activities to the KEEP platform during July 2023. The platform generates structured volunteered geographic information (VGI), 
including detailed ride-level information such as trajectory ID, trip duration, distance, and start and end times. KEEP-based trajectory 
data have been increasingly applied across various research fields, including trajectory pattern mining (Li et al., 2025), the relationship 
between the built environment and active travel (Zhang, S. et al., 2024), and exercise intensity analysis (Qiu et al., 2024), underscoring 
their growing value and potential for urban cycling behavior research. Trips recorded on KEEP are more likely to reflect exercise, 
leisure, or discretionary cycling behaviors. This potential sampling bias is common to many fitness-tracking datasets (e.g., Strava) and
