A B S T R A C T
Urban characteristics can shape physical activity, but evidence regarding their joint role alongside individual 
perceptions remains limited. This study examined the combined associations of multiple environmental char­
acteristics with physical activity across the five most populous cities in Bulgaria. A cross-sectional survey of 4640 
adults was conducted in 2023. Physical activity across different intensity levels was measured with the short 
form of the International Physical Activity Questionnaire and linked to objective measures of neighborhood 
walkability, fine particulate matter, road traffic noise, area-level socioeconomic status (SES), and availability of 
greenspace and bluespace within 300 m and 1000 m buffers. Presence of a home garden was self-reported, along 
with perceived walkability, bikeability, greenspace, bluespace, and sociodemographic factors. Results showed 
that walkability, greenspace, area-level SES, home gardens, perceived bluespace, high/low perceived greenspace 
(vs. medium), and bikeability were positively associated with time spent in vigorous physical activity. Walk­
ability, greenspace, perceived bluespace, low/high perceived greenspace, home gardens, and bluespace were 
positively associated with moderate-to-vigorous physical activity. Objective and perceived walkability, medium/ 
high levels of greenspace, low/high area-level SES, and home gardens were positively associated with walking. 
Associations remained robust after accounting for air pollution and noise, which were unexpectedly positively 
associated with certain forms of physical activity. These findings appear meaningful from a preventive 
perspective and may provide arguments for improving urban design conducive to physical activity and urban 
planning initiatives.

Methods
2.1. Survey design and sampling
Between August and October 2023, a representative quota sample of 
the general population was collected from the five most populous cities 
in Bulgaria. The study areas included the capital, Sofia (~1.2 million 
inhabitants), as well as Plovdiv (~330 000), Varna (~320 000), Burgas 
(~190 000), and Ruse (~120 000). Sofia, Plovdiv, and Ruse are land­
locked with varying access to rivers (e.g., Iskar, Maritsa, and the Dan­
ube), whereas Varna and Burgas are located on the Black Sea coast. A 
map of the study areas is shown in Fig. S1 in Supplementary File 1.
A detailed description of the Bulgarian Healthy Urban Environment 
study is provided elsewhere (Dzhambov et al., 2025). Briefly, partici­
pants were approached at their homes by trained interviewers from a 
professional survey company. Bulgarian-speaking adults aged ≥18 years 
residing at their current address for at least one year were included. 
Participants were sampled according to quotas from predefined spatial 
typologies within each city to improve spatial contrast and thus, in­
crease analytical power. A total of 4640 participants were included, with 
response rates ranging from 30% in Sofia to 58% in Plovdiv.
Participants provided informed consent verbally. All procedures 
followed relevant EU data protection regulations. The study was 
approved by the Scientific Ethics Committee at the Medical University of 
Plovdiv (Protocol No. 4/May 04, 2023; Opinion No. P-1253/May 17, 
2023).
2.2. Physical activity
The International Physical Activity Questionnaire-Short Form 
(IPAQ-SF) was used to assess participants’ physical activity across 
different intensity levels (Craig et al., 2003). Participants were instruc­
ted to recall a typical most recent week, during which they were neither 
sick nor travelling out of the city. Vigorous physical activity (VPA) was 
defined as activity performed for at least 10 min at a time that required 
substantial physical effort and resulted in breathing much harder than 
normal (e.g., lifting heavy weights, fast running, or fast cycling). 
Moderate-intensity physical activity (MPA) was defined as activity 
lasting at least 10 min that required moderate physical effort and caused

breathing somewhat harder than normal (e.g., carrying light loads or 
cycling at a normal pace). Walking was defined as any walking for at 
least 10 min at a time, including walking at work or at home, for 
transportation, during leisure time, or while engaging in sports.
Data processing involved converting all reported activity durations 
to minutes and excluding implausible values in accordance with IPAQ 
scoring guidelines (IPAQ Research Committee). Participants reporting 
total physical activity that exceeded 960 min per day were excluded. 
Reported activity durations of less than 10 min were recoded to 0 min 
because very short bouts of physical activity are less likely to activate 
metabolic pathways, and durations greater than 180 min were truncated 
to 180 min because they were considered unlikely.
Outcome variables were constructed from self-reported frequency 
(days per week) and duration (minutes per day) of physical activity. 
Compliance with established physical activity guidelines (WHO, 2020) 
was operationalized with dichotomous indicators: ≥75 min per week of 
VPA, and ≥150 min per week of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity 
(MPA + 2*VPA = MVPA), and walking for ≥150 min per week. MVPA 
was calculated as the sum of MPA plus twice the time spent in VPA, 
consistent with the WHO equivalence assumption (WHO, 2020) and 
IPAQ MET-based scoring (IPAQ Research Committee). Additionally, 
continuous measures of VPA, MPA, and walking time were computed in 
minutes per week among participants reporting non-zero activity levels.
2.3. Environmental characteristics
Participants’ residential environment was characterized using both 
objective measures and self-reported perceptions, as these provide 
complementary information. The literature and data availability 
informed the choice of physical environmental characteristics (Smith 
et al., 2017).
2.3.1. Objective neighborhood characteristics
Walkability refers to the extent to which the built environment en­
courages walking (Gao et al., 2020). It was calculated using 2021 census 
population density data, Shannon's index for land use diversity derived 
from 2018 European Urban Atlas (Copernicus Land Monitoring Service, 
2021), land use/land cover and cadastral data (GCCA, 2016), and 
OpenStreetMap-derived (OpenStreetMap contributors, 2023) four-way 
intersection density (Dzhambov et al., 2025). The z-scores of these 
three input variables were summarized within circular buffers of 300 m 
and 1000 m. These buffer sizes correspond to typical walking distances 
near one's home, considering the size of the study areas, and fall within a 
range deemed relevant to physical activity (Browning and Lee, 2017; 
Wang et al., 2021; Zare Sakhvidi et al., 2025).
Mean annual concentrations of particulate matter with aerodynamic 
diameter ≤2.5 μm (PM2.5) were obtained for the year 2019 at a 25 m 
spatial resolution from a geographically weighted land use regression 
model based on measured pollutants at monitoring stations across 
Europe within the EXPANSE project (Shen et al., 2022). PM2.5 levels 
were predicted from road and land use variables, satellite retrievals, and 
chemical transport model estimates, with a five-fold cross-validation R2 
of 0.77 (Shen et al., 2022). PM2.5 was used as an indicator of air 
pollution rather than NO2 because the latter showed a strong correlation
