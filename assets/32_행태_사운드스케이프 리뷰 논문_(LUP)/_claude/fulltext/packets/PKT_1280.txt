Abstract
Rural public spaces are crucial to the daily activities of older adults; however, limited
research has examined the effects of their environmental characteristics on older adults’
spatiotemporal behavior and perception from a multisensory perspective. This study
hypothesizes that composite sensory environments have significant nonlinear predictive
effects on older adults’ behavior types and satisfaction. In this study, 10 sample spaces
were selected in Qingdao, China. Multi-source data were collected through a two-week
period of unobtrusive observation and subjective questionnaire surveys (N = 241). Multiple
logistic regression was used to analyze the main effects of environmental characteristics,
and an MLP model with a single hidden layer of 100 units was constructed to predict dwell
time and satisfaction. The results show that, in the investigated rural context, older adults’
dominant behavior was social activity (81.12%), which mainly occurred in built spaces such
as squares. Multiple logistic regression indicated that, among the various environmental
factors, visual aesthetics had a statistically significant effect on behavior types (p = 0.013).
The MLP model achieved prediction accuracies of 85.3% for dwell time and 93.1% for
satisfaction. The key predictive variables were volume perception (100% importance), the
Natural Sound Index (NSI) (92.1%), and visual aesthetics (89.3%). Subgroup heterogeneity
analysis further showed that older-old adults and those with poorer health conditions
were more sensitive to pavement quality and physical comfort, whereas older adults living
alone or with limited household companionship were more strongly influenced by visual
aesthetics and natural soundscape quality. The theoretical significance of this study lies in
proposing quantitative measures of natural sound and odor indices and revealing that, in
the specific northern rural built environment, the coordinated design of visual and auditory
environments plays an important role in improving spatial quality. The findings provide
empirical support for the age-friendly micro-renewal of rural public spaces in specific
regions. However, due to the limitations of single-season data and a relatively small sample
size, their generalizability needs to be further verified across regions.

1. Introduction
The intensification of rapid urbanization and population mobility has led rural China
to face more severe aging challenges than urban areas [1]. Data from the Seventh National
Population Census show that the proportion of people aged 60 years and above in ru-
ral China has reached 23.81%, which is significantly higher than that in urban areas [2].
Against this background, rural public spaces serve as core settings for older adults’ daily
social interaction, emotional attachment, and physical exercise. Their age-friendly envi-
ronmental quality is directly related to the health and well-being of rural residents and
the quality of aging in place [3,4]. However, due to long-standing “urban-biased” poli-
cies, the theory and practice of age-friendly public spaces have mainly focused on urban
communities [5,6], while the micro-environmental construction of rural public spaces has
often been marginalized [7–9].
In recent years, some scholars have begun to focus on age-friendliness in rural spaces,
but existing studies still show clear dimensional limitations [3]. A large body of literature
mainly focuses on the construction of older adults’ life circles at the macro level, the
spatial allocation of elderly care service facilities, or neighborhood network support from a
sociological perspective [8,9]. Although these studies emphasize the macro-level necessity
of rural age-friendliness, most of them overlook the specific mechanisms through which
the physical characteristics of the micro-scale built environment influence older adults’
high-frequency daily spatiotemporal behavior [10]. Rural society is essentially a typical
“acquaintance society”, and the outdoor activities of older adults often arise from the
need for functional gathering [7]. Therefore, analyzing the mapping relationship between
objective physical environmental characteristics, older adults’ spatiotemporal behavior,
and subjective perception at the micro-spatial scale remains an important blind spot that
urgently needs to be addressed in current rural living environment research [11,12].
In the field of micro-environmental behavior, human spatial perception is not based
on information received through a single dimension, but is a composite process formed
by the coordinated action of multiple sensory channels, including vision, hearing, smell,
and touch [13,14]. Environmental stimuli, as independent variables, trigger different
receptors in the cerebral cortex, regulate older adults’ psychological emotions and cognitive

Methods
2.1. Case Study Area
This study selected two typical rural villages, Xiaolingzi Village and Xishiling Village,
in the West Coast New Area of Qingdao, China, as the study area (Figure 1). The aging
rate in rural Qingdao has reached 20.28% [23]. Due to the traditional concept of “aging
in place”, older adults in these areas have a very low willingness to migrate, making the
study area highly typical and representative [24].
In terms of sample selection, five public spaces were selected in each village, resulting
in a total of 10 sample spaces. These included spaces under trees, squares, areas in front of
service centers, roadways, and spaces surrounding houses. The selection criteria were as
follows: (1) full public accessibility; and (2) the presence of regular daily activities by older
adults. Although the number of spatial samples (N = 10) was relatively small in terms of ge-
ographic scale, this study adopted high-frequency and long-term behavioral tracking over
14 consecutive days, with observations conducted for 14 h per day. Dense time-series data
were used to compensate for the limited number of spatial samples, thereby ensuring that
the study could capture high-resolution micro-scale environment–behavior relationships.
The target group of this study was defined as middle-aged and older adults in rural
areas. The inclusion of some participants aged 55–60 was based on the practical considera-
tion that this “young-old” group has already entered an aging-in-place lifestyle under the
changing rural labor structure in China.
The data collection process of this study, including unobtrusive behavioral observation
and questionnaire surveys, was approved by the Ethics Committee of Qingdao University
(QDU-HEC-2022150). All questionnaire surveys were conducted with informed consent
from the participants. The unobtrusive observation recorded only group behavioral charac-

2.2. Data and Variables
The data collection period was from 11–24 September 2022, excluding rainy days
and other extreme weather conditions. During this period, environmental monitoring,
behavioral recording, and subjective questionnaire surveys were conducted simultaneously.
2.2.1. Rural Environmental Characteristics
The rural built environment is composite in nature and can be categorized into
four types according to sensory stimulation: tactile, auditory, visual, and olfactory
environments [25]. Based on this classification, 11 representative environmental character-
istics were selected. These indicators were chosen because they are relatively easy to obtain,
have high reliability, and may affect the emotions and activities of older adults, as shown
in Table 1.

To ensure the scientific validity and reliability of the data, all environmental moni-
toring equipment was calibrated before daily measurements. Air temperature, relative
humidity, and wind speed were measured using a Testo 480 multifunctional instrument.
The monitoring points were uniformly set at a height of 1.5 m above the ground, corre-
sponding to the breathing zone of older adults. A 5-min average value was used to smooth
short-term fluctuations. Acoustic data were collected using an AWA5636 sound level meter
to measure the equivalent continuous A-weighted sound pressure level (LAeq) over 5 min.
