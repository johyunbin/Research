Abstract
Sewage odour is a common pollution odour source produced by urban water bodies and
negatively impacts people’s behaviour. This study aimed to explore whether sound could change
this negative effect. Therefore, a behaviour-observation experiment was conducted in a typical
urban waterfront park using the sewage odour as the source of the pollution smell and playing
typical sound sources in urban environments (music and fan sounds) with and without odour. The
results showed that for crowd paths, music could significantly attract a crowd, fan sound or
sewage odour could keep the crowd away. Furthermore, when there was sewage odour, the effects
of the trends of approaching or receding from the sound sources caused by the music and fan
sounds, respectively, have been strengthened. Sound and odour had relatively independent impacts
on the crowd speed and duration time, reflected in the fact that music significantly reduced the
crowd speed whereas fan sound and sewage odour significantly improved it. Music significantly
increased the crowd duration time, while fan sound and sewage odour significantly reduced it. On
this basis, the independent effects of sound and odour were superimposed, and the positive effect
of music was stronger than the negative effect of sewage odour.
Keywords: sounds, sewage odour, crowd behaviour, multisensory, urban environments.

1 Introduction
Senses are the media that connect one’s perception with the environment. Urban
environments have rich sensory information and exert an imperceptible influence on people.
Improving sensory environments has become an important means of enhancing the quality of
urban environments [1]. Vision, audition, and olfaction are the main media of perceiving the
environment, the processing of environmental information by them is sometimes impossible to be
ignored. People’s eyes, ears, and noses constantly receive information from the bottom up,
regardless of whether they have subjective will [2]. Therefore, vision, audition, and olfaction play
important roles in connecting people’s perceptions and urban environments. Compared with the
urban visual and auditory environments, people often ignore the olfactory environment.
Odour is an important feature of the olfactory environment, yet it is an aspect that is often
overlooked. Because when people become accustomed to the odour of an environment, they often
ignore its existence, and sometimes it is difficult for people to recognise the type of odour,
however the impacts of odour on people are still present. Odour types in urban environments are
classified according to their sources normally, mainly including plant, food, pollution,
construction-material, human, and animal odours [3-5]. Among these, negative pollution odours
commonly occur in urban environments and are the type about which people most often complain
[6,7]; thus many studies focus on the subjective evaluation and treatment of negative odours such
as those from automobile exhaust, sewage, livestock farming, and so on [8–12]. Perception and
behaviour are inseparable; behaviour, as the explicit performance of psychological perception,
also plays an important role in sensory environmental assessment because surrounding people’s
activities and behaviours constitute a key component of the environment [13,14]. Behaviour can
be divided into individual and crowd behaviours, individual behaviour generally refers to a
person’s attitude or performance under specific circumstances. In contrast to individual behaviour,
which is somewhat random [15], crowd behaviour refers to a crowd’s performance in an
environment, which always follows certain rules [16,17]. Especially for different environmental
conditions, individual behaviours can often be synthesized into relatively simple indicators of
crowd behaviour, which can better reflect the overall behavioural regularities of the crowds under

Considering the types and availability of odour source as well as the actual site conditions,
sewage odour, being a typical pollution odour in urban environments, was selected as the variable
for this study. Because sewage odour is usually the result of poor management of certain waters,
while its visual environment is attractive to many people. Thus study of crowd behaviour for this
area helps balance this contradiction. In addition, regarding the experimental-site selection criteria,
the area had to have a dominant odour that was relatively stable, when there is an odour, it can be
smelled in the entire area; odour conditions must be variable at the same time period to achieve
different intensities; there should be no interference from other odours in the surrounding area; the
sound pressure level (SPL) and sound source type of the background sound environment should be
stable; and there should be no special and sudden sound source in the surrounding area; adequate
behavioral samples are available in the region.
The study site was located in an urban waterfront park called Anyanghe park, in Daoli
District, Harbin, China. The sewage odour came from the sewage discharged from the river in the
park. Preliminary site survey found that due to the different drainage capacity, there was no
pollution odour perceived when the water level was high; however, the sewage odour could be
smelled when the water level was low. And in the case of almost no wind, the subjective intensity
of odour is stable during the same period of time. Therefore the experiments for the two odour
conditions were carried out on two separate days with different water levels. The study area is
shown in Fig. 1. It was the walkway in the park, which is approximately 40 meters long and 4
meters wide. When the sewage odour occurred, it could cover the whole area; when it did not, the
odour could not be smelled in the area.

2.2 Sound source selection
Following previous studies, the current one selected fan and music sounds [33], as typical
sound sources in urban environments, as the sound variables, which represent the common sounds
in the urban environments that bring negative and positive perceptions respectively [37,38]. And
they are both artificial sounds and point sources so that they can be played through a loudspeaker.
A cooling fan controls the ambient temperature of equipment and is necessary for the normal
operation of air conditioners, automobile engines, and daily electronic equipment; thus, the sound
produced by a cooling fan is a typical and continuous sound source in urban environments [39].
Before the formal experiment, fan sounds in various urban environments were selected and 42
subjects surveyed through a 7-point Likert scale questionnaire to investigate the degree of
annoyance of these fan sounds. Finally, the fan sound with the highest annoyance degree ( > 6 on
the scale) was selected as the experimental sound source.
Light music without lyrics was chosen as the music type. The speed (beats per minutes or
bpm) of music is among its important characteristics. Research has defined 40–70 bpm, 85–110
bpm, and 120 bpm or above as slow, medium, and fast speeds, respectively [40]. A music speed
below 80 bpm is associated with negative emotions, while that above 120 bpm is associated with
positive emotions [41]. Therefore, it is more appropriate to choose medium-speed music. The final
music chosen for the study was Annie’s Wonderland, with a speed of 100.99 bpm and a high
ranking online in terms of listening pleasure.
There were three sound conditions in this study: no playing sound, playing fan sound, and
playing music. The location of the loudspeaker playing the sound was considered: sound could be
heard anywhere in the area; the distance between the front of the loudspeaker and any reflector
was at least 1.5 meters [42]; to avoid any visual impact on the loudspeaker, it was hidden in the
study area [13]. Based on this, the loudspeaker was placed behind a bush 5 metres from the edge
of the study area (see Figure 2 for exact location).
