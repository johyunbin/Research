## Title

Soundscape and human behaviour in urban public open spaces: a systematic review
of bidirectional relationships

(alternates: "How acoustic environments shape observable human behaviour in urban
and landscape spaces: a systematic review and partial meta-analysis")

## Review question(s)

- RQ1 (forward): Which characteristics of outdoor acoustic environments (sound source
  type, level, composition, and soundscape interventions) are associated with which
  observable human behaviours in urban public open spaces?
- RQ1b (reverse): How do users' behaviours and activities shape the soundscape itself
  and its perception/assessment?
- RQ2: What are the direction and magnitude of these effects across behavioural
  domains? (meta-analysis where effect sizes are extractable)
- RQ3: How have behavioural measurement methods evolved (self-report → on-site
  observation/behavioural mapping → sensors, GPS, mobile phone data, computer vision),
  and do conclusions differ by measurement method?
- RQ4: What are the research gaps and implications for landscape and urban planning?

## Searches

- Databases: Web of Science Core Collection; Scopus; PubMed. Supplementary: OpenAlex.
- Search dates: PubMed and OpenAlex executed 2 August 2026 (213 and 873 records
  respectively); WoS and Scopus to be executed [date].
- Restrictions: English language; peer-reviewed journal articles only (conference
  proceedings, book chapters, dissertations, and grey literature excluded); no date limit.
- Supplementary methods: backward and forward citation tracking of included studies;
  screening of reference lists of adjacent reviews.
- Full Boolean strings for all databases are archived in the project repository
  (three concept blocks: [A] acoustic environment/soundscape terms AND [B] behaviour
  terms AND [C] urban/landscape setting terms).

## Condition or domain being studied

Human behaviour in outdoor and semi-outdoor urban public open spaces (parks, streets,
squares/plazas, waterfronts, campuses, recreational areas) in relation to the acoustic
environment (soundscape).

## Participants/population

General population users of urban public open spaces. Studies of exclusively clinical
populations are excluded. Animal behaviour studies (soundscape ecology) are excluded.

## Exposure(s)/Intervention(s)

Acoustic environment characteristics: sound source types (traffic, natural sounds,
birdsong, water, human sounds, music), sound levels, soundscape quality/composition,
and deliberate soundscape interventions (e.g., added sound installations).
For the reverse direction (RQ1b): user behaviours/activities as the exposure shaping
soundscape formation and its assessment.

## Comparator(s)/control

No sound / alternative sound conditions / different exposure levels in experimental
studies; exposure gradients in observational studies.

## Types of study to be included

Empirical peer-reviewed studies: field and laboratory experiments (including VR),
field observations (behavioural mapping, covert observation), natural experiments,
and sensor/big-data observational studies (GPS, mobile phone data, video/computer
vision). Excluded: pure simulations (e.g., agent-based models without empirical
behavioural data), reviews, commentaries.

## Context

Outdoor and semi-outdoor urban/landscape public spaces. Excluded: indoor commercial
settings (retail/hospitality background-music literature is treated as background
only), workplaces, hospitals, vehicles (driving behaviour).

## Main outcome(s)

Observable (measured or observed) human behaviours, organized in five domains:
1. Movement (walking speed, gait/rhythm, route choice, approach/avoidance)
2. Staying (dwell time, lingering, sitting/standing, stopping)
3. Space use (visitation, frequency of use, seat/location choice, spatial distribution)
4. Social behaviour (paired/group interactions, prosocial/antisocial behaviour,
   conversation, crowd behaviour)
5. Activity (physical activity, recreation activity type and diversity)

Excluded as primary outcomes: perception/annoyance-only, preference-only,
behavioural intention-only, and physiological-only outcomes (intention examined
in sensitivity analysis only).

## Data extraction (selection and coding)

Two-stage screening (title/abstract, then full text) with reasons for exclusion
recorded at full-text stage (PRISMA 2020). Extraction sheet: study design, setting,
country, sample, acoustic exposure and its measurement, behavioural outcome and its
measurement method (self-report / observation / sensor-based), direction
(soundscape→behaviour or behaviour→soundscape), effect estimates where available.
Screening tool: Rayyan [or ASReview]. [Single reviewer with AI-assisted second
check / two independent reviewers — 확정 필요]

## Risk of bias (quality) assessment

Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT 2018) across heterogeneous designs; RoB 2 for
randomized experimental subsets where applicable. Quality used descriptively and
in sensitivity analyses, not as an exclusion criterion.

## Strategy for data synthesis

Structured narrative synthesis following SWiM guidance, organized by the five
behavioural domains × sound source categories (evidence map / harvest plot).
Random-effects meta-analysis for homogeneous clusters where ≥3 comparable effect
sizes exist (anticipated: walking speed; dwell time/lingering), using Hedges' g or
correlation r, with heterogeneity (I²) and sensitivity analyses. A conceptual
framework linking acoustic environment, behaviour, and measurement generations
will be developed for landscape planning application.

## Analysis of subgroups or subsets

By behavioural domain; sound source category; setting type (park vs street vs
square); study design (experiment vs observation); measurement method generation
(self-report vs observation vs sensor/big-data); direction (forward vs reverse).
